It's late autumn, and many students will soon hear the same message from friends, agents or a group chat: *sign for next year's house now or miss out.*

This year the pressure deserves a second look. This is the first house-hunting season since the **Renters' Rights Act** started applying to tenancies in England on **1 May 2026**, and it changed many of the rules students relied on. Contracts work differently, notice works differently, and one detail about *when* you sign could affect whether your landlord can ask you to leave next summer.

This guide covers **England only**. Wales and Scotland have their own systems, so if you're studying there, check your university's housing service.

## Step 1: Work out what kind of housing you're looking at

The Act treats different types of student housing very differently. Before reading anything else, work out which category your place falls into.

| Housing type | Fixed-term contract? | Can landlord use the student ground (Ground 4A)? | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| University halls | Yes, usually (exempt) | Not relevant | Normal halls contract terms |
| Private halls / PBSA signed up to an approved code | Yes (exempt) | Not relevant | Is the provider a member of the code? |
| Shared house or flat with 3+ unrelated students (HMO) | No, open-ended tenancy | Yes, if all conditions are met | Written Ground 4A notice, signing date |
| 1–2 bed flat or house (not an HMO) | No, open-ended tenancy | No | Notice terms, other possession grounds |

Take each row in turn.

### Halls and private halls (PBSA)

University-owned accommodation and most purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) are **exempt**, as long as the provider has signed up to an approved code. The main ones are the **ANUK/Unipol National Code** and the **UUK Code of Practice**. Exempt providers can still use fixed-term contracts, and the [University of London housing guide](https://stories.london.ac.uk/private-housing-guide/private-housing-guide/finding-a-property/renters-rights-act/index.html) notes that private halls can still ask for large amounts of rent in advance.

**Practical tip:** don't assume a glossy private hall is covered. Ask the provider directly which code it belongs to, and check that on the code's own website before you sign. [Forsters](https://www.forsters.co.uk/feature/renters-rights-act-hub/students) explains how the code exemption works in more detail.

### Shared houses (HMOs)

Most second- and third-year students end up here. A house shared by **three or more unrelated tenants** is a house in multiple occupation (HMO). These are now ordinary private tenancies under the new rules, so most of this article applies to them.

### One- and two-bed lets

If you and a partner or best friend are renting a one- or two-bed flat that isn't an HMO (and isn't part of one), the student possession ground **doesn't apply**. According to [Unipol](https://www.unipol.org.uk/advice/renters-rights-act-advice-for-students/renters-rights-act-faqs-for-students/) and the [University of Birmingham landlord FAQ](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/study/accommodation/living-in-the-community/the-renters-rights-act-landlord-faqs), the landlord would have to wait for you to give notice, or rely on another legal ground such as serious rent arrears.

## What changed on 1 May 2026

If you've rented privately before, forget some of what you learned. According to the [NRLA](https://www.nrla.org.uk/resources/student-lettings), since 1 May 2026 landlords in England can no longer:

- offer **assured shorthold tenancies (ASTs)**
- offer **fixed terms** (such as '12 months from 1 July')
- serve **Section 21** 'no-fault' eviction notices

Existing ASTs automatically became **periodic assured tenancies** on that date.

In practice:

1. **Your tenancy is open-ended.** It continues until you give notice or the landlord uses a valid legal ground for possession ([University of Birmingham student FAQs](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/study/accommodation/living-in-the-community/the-renters-rights-act-student-faqs)).
2. **You can give two months' notice at any point in the year.** You're no longer locked in for a full 12 months. If you're sharing a joint tenancy, how notice works when only one housemate wants to leave can be more complicated, so get advice before acting.
3. **Landlords can be fined for breaking the rules.** Councils can fine landlords for prohibited conduct: **up to £7,000 for a first breach and up to £40,000 for serious or repeated breaches** ([Landlord Studio](https://www.landlordstudio.com/uk-blog/ground-4a-student-possession-renters-rights-act)).

## Ground 4A: the new 'student ground', explained

The end of fixed terms worried student landlords. Their business depends on one group moving out in summer so the next group can move in. To deal with this, the Act created **Ground 4A**, a possession ground just for student houses.

Ground 4A is **mandatory**. That means a court *must* grant possession if every condition is met. But the conditions are strict, and **all** of them must apply. Based on the summary from law firm [Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/student-accommodation-and-the-renters-rights-act-2025):

1. **The property must be an HMO**, with at least three unrelated tenants.
2. **Every tenant must meet the 'student test'.** They are full-time students, or the landlord reasonably believes they will be.
3. **The landlord must tell you in writing, before the tenancy is entered into**, that they intend to rely on Ground 4A.
4. **The tenancy must not have been entered into more than six months before** you're entitled to move in.
5. **The landlord must give at least four months' notice**, and that notice must **expire between 1 June and 30 September**.

If even one condition isn't met, the landlord can't use Ground 4A. They would need a different legal ground, or would have to wait for you to give notice.

**One transitional point:** for tenants already living in a property on 1 May 2026, some sources (such as [RentalBux](https://rentalbux.com/blogs/student-renters-and-renters-rights-act-university-housing)) say the landlord had to serve written notice of intent to rely on Ground 4A by **31 May 2026**, or the ground isn't available for this cycle. If that affects your current house, check it with your university's housing service.

## Why you probably don't need to sign in November

This is the part that matters most right now.

Look at condition 4 again. The tenancy must not have been entered into **more than six months before** the move-in date. For a typical **September 2027** move-in, that points to signing no earlier than around **March 2027**.

So if a landlord signs you up in October or November 2026 for a September 2027 start, they **appear to lose the right to use Ground 4A** for that tenancy. Two things follow:

- **Landlords have less reason to push you into signing early.** A sensible student landlord who wants to use Ground 4A will want to sign within the six-month window. The 'sign before Christmas or you'll be homeless' message makes less sense than it used to. [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/rent-reforms-slowing-down-student-rush-housing) has reported on the reforms slowing the student housing rush.
- **If you do sign this early, you may have more security.** Without Ground 4A, the landlord can't use the student ground to make you leave in summer. They would need another valid ground.

**An important caveat:** sources word this condition differently. The University of London guide describes it as six months having passed since the tenancy began, which isn't the same thing. We've followed Mishcon's wording, which matches the condition as written in the statute. Before you make decisions based on this, **check the official page** or ask your students' union advice centre to confirm how it applies to you.

Be aware too that housing markets differ by city. In some places, agents may still push early signing, or offer things like 'holding agreements'. Don't let urgency rush you into signing anything you don't understand.

## Your checklist before you sign anything

Work through this with your housemates before anyone hands over money.

1. **Identify the housing type.** Halls, code-registered PBSA, HMO or a 1–2 bed let? Use the table above.
2. **For PBSA: confirm code membership.** Ask which code the provider has signed up to (ANUK/Unipol National Code or UUK Code of Practice) and check it independently.
3. **For HMOs: look for a written Ground 4A statement.** Has the landlord told you in writing, *before* signing, that they intend to rely on Ground 4A? Keep a copy.
4. **Check the dates.** When will the tenancy be entered into, and when are you entitled to move in? Is the gap more than six months?
5. **Read the notice terms.** Your tenancy should reflect your right to give two months' notice. If a contract claims to lock you into a fixed term on a private HMO let, ask questions.
6. **Ask how the group's tenancy is set up.** Is it a joint tenancy or individual contracts? This affects what happens if someone drops out or wants to leave.
7. **Get the contract checked.** Most universities and students' unions offer free contract checks. Use them.
8. **Don't let pressure decide for you.** If an agent says 'it'll be gone tomorrow', take a breath. Under the new rules, early signing often helps the landlord less than it used to.

## Quick FAQ

**Are fixed-term contracts gone completely?**
Not completely. They're gone for most private rentals, including shared student houses. University halls and PBSA providers signed up to an approved code (ANUK/Unipol or UUK) are exempt and can still use fixed terms.

**Can my landlord make me leave in the summer?**
Only with a valid legal ground. For student HMOs, that usually means Ground 4A, and only if all five conditions are met. These include written notice before signing, at least four months' notice expiring between 1 June and 30 September, and signing no more than six months before move-in.

**I'm renting a two-bed flat with one friend. Does Ground 4A apply?**
No. One- and two-bed lets that aren't HMOs are excluded. The landlord would need to wait for your notice or use another ground, such as serious rent arrears.

**Can I leave early if my plans change?**
In a private tenancy covered by the Act, you can give two months' notice at any time. Joint tenancies can be more complicated, so speak to your housing advice service first.

**Do these rules apply in Scotland or Wales?**
No. This guide covers England only. Scotland and Wales have separate systems.

## Next steps

Before your group commits to anything for 2027–28, sit down together and agree which type of housing you want. Then book a free contract check with your university or students' union and use the checklist above with every landlord or agent you speak to. Ask directly whether they plan to rely on Ground 4A, ask to see it in writing, and compare the signing date with your move-in date. If something doesn't add up, or someone is pushing you to sign fast, pause and get advice. Under the new rules, taking your time is often the smarter move.
