If you hold an offer from a Canadian college or university for January or September 2027, the next step is the study permit. The rules have changed a lot in the past few years. There is now a national cap, an extra provincial letter and a higher savings requirement. This guide explains each piece, what is confirmed and what you should check yourself before you apply.

**A note on accuracy:** Canadian immigration rules change often, sometimes with only a few weeks' notice. Where a figure comes from secondary sources rather than an official Government of Canada page, we say so. Always confirm amounts, fees and dates on Canada.ca before you submit anything.

## Do you need a study permit?

According to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), you need a study permit for any academic, professional or vocational programme that lasts **longer than six months**. That covers most degrees, diplomas and college certificates. IRCC's own summary is in its [2026 transition binder on the International Student Program](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/transparency/transition-binders/deputy-minister-2026/international-student-program.html).

If your course is six months or shorter, the rules are different. Check the official page for your situation.

## The four core requirements

Most applicants need these four things:

1. **A letter of acceptance** from a designated learning institution (DLI). This is a school approved to host international students.
2. **A study permit application** submitted to IRCC.
3. **Proof of funds** showing you can pay tuition, travel and living costs.
4. **A provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL/TAL)**, unless you are exempt.

Legal guides such as [BridgePoint Law's study permit guide](https://bridgepointlaw.ca/canadian-study-permit-guide/) also stress two softer factors that officers weigh: evidence of **ties to your home country** and a **clear, believable plan** for after your studies. A study plan that explains why this programme, why Canada and how it fits your career goals can strengthen a borderline application.

## The cap: what "150,000" really means

Canada limits how many new international students it admits each year. Secondary sources, including [this 2027 application guide from MapleVisa](https://maplevisa.ca/apply-for-canada-study-permit-2027/), describe the figure for new student arrivals as about **150,000**. The important point is that this is a **planning target**, not a guarantee. It does not tell you how many permits IRCC will approve or how many applications it will accept.

The 2027 targets were due to be confirmed or adjusted by 1 November 2026. Before you rely on any number, read the government's [Supplementary Information for the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/immigration-system/levels/supplementary-immigration-levels-2027-2029.html) for the final figures.

**What it means for you in practice:** the cap mostly works through the PAL/TAL system. Each province and territory gets a share of spaces and hands them out to schools. If your school has an offer for you, it usually has access to PAL spaces too. But spaces are limited, so don't sit on your offer.

## The PAL/TAL letter

A PAL (provincial attestation letter) or TAL (territorial attestation letter) confirms that a province or territory has given you one of its spaces under the cap. IRCC says you must include one with your study permit application **unless you are exempt**.

### Who is exempt?

IRCC's official text gives this example: **graduate students in degree-granting programmes at public DLIs** (for example, master's and PhD students at public universities). News coverage, such as [this VisaHQ report](https://www.visahq.com/news/2026-01-10/ca/graduate-students-exempt-from-study-permit-cap-as-ircc-drops-provincial-attestation-letter-requirement/), also describes graduate students being exempted from the cap and the PAL requirement.

The full exemption list is longer and can change. **Check the current list on Canada.ca** before you assume you're exempt. If you're a graduate student at a private institution, or in a non-degree graduate certificate or diploma, don't assume the exemption covers you.

### How to get a PAL

The process differs by province, but the general pattern is:

1. Accept your offer and pay any deposit your school asks for.
2. Request the PAL/TAL through your school. In most provinces the school requests it for you or sends you a form.
3. Wait for the letter, then attach it to your study permit application.

Ask your admissions office three things: how they issue PALs, how long it takes and whether spaces are running low for your intake.

### Quebec is different

If you're studying in Quebec, you also need a **Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ)** from the provincial government, as well as the attestation letter. Build extra time into your plan, because you usually need the CAQ before you can apply for the federal study permit.

## Proof of funds: the $23,448 rule

This is where many applications go wrong. Multiple secondary sources, including [Moving2Canada's report on the increase](https://moving2canada.com/2026/08/proof-of-funds-requirement-for-canadian-study-permit-increasing/), say that from **1 September 2026** a single applicant must show **CAD 23,448** for living costs. That amount is **on top of** your first year's tuition and your travel costs.

Some sources list a different, higher figure for Quebec, which sets its own requirements. Extra amounts apply if family members come with you. **Confirm the exact amounts and effective date on IRCC's proof-of-funds page** before you prepare your documents.

### A worked example

Say your first-year tuition is CAD 30,000. You'd need to show roughly:

- CAD 30,000 for tuition
- CAD 23,448 for living costs
- Plus your travel costs

That's more than CAD 53,000 before flights. Officers also look at **where the money came from** and how long it has been in the account. A large deposit that appears suddenly with no explanation can raise questions.

Common forms of proof include:

- Bank statements in your name or a sponsor's name
- A Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) from a participating Canadian bank
- An education loan approval letter
- Scholarship or funding letters
- Receipts showing tuition you've already paid (this reduces how much you still need to show)

## Working while you study

Secondary sources, including the [SchoolsCanada 2027 rules summary](https://www.schoolscanada.ca/canada-study-permit-rules-2027), say eligible students can work off campus up to **24 hours a week during academic terms**. They cite the Canada.ca "Work off campus" page, updated 15 April 2026. Rules for scheduled breaks are different. Check the official page for the current limits and eligibility conditions.

Two cautions:

- **Don't build your budget around work income.** Your proof of funds has to cover your costs without a job, and visa officers assess it that way.
- **Breaking the work limit can affect your status.** Keep a record of your hours.

## After graduation: PGWP and family

The **post-graduation work permit (PGWP)** lets eligible graduates work in Canada after their studies. Eligibility depends on factors such as your programme, your institution and, for some applicants, field-of-study and language requirements. These rules have changed recently, so read the official PGWP page **before you choose your programme**, not after you graduate. Not every programme leads to a PGWP.

If you have a spouse or partner, ask whether they qualify for a **spousal open work permit**. Eligibility has been narrowed and depends on your programme level, so check the current rules on Canada.ca.

## A step-by-step timeline

1. **Now:** Accept your offer and confirm your DLI number.
2. **Immediately after:** Ask your school about the PAL/TAL (or confirm you're exempt). Quebec students should start the CAQ.
3. **In parallel:** Gather proof of funds, getting statements that show a stable history, plus your passport, study plan and any language test results.
4. **Check processing times** for your country on Canada.ca. They vary widely.
5. **Apply online** and pay the fees. Book biometrics as soon as you get the instruction letter.
6. **Respond quickly** to any request for more documents or a medical exam.
7. **After approval:** You'll get a letter of introduction (and a visa or eTA if needed). The study permit itself is issued when you arrive in Canada.

## Quick FAQ

**I'm doing a master's at a public university. Do I need a PAL?**
IRCC lists graduate students in degree-granting programmes at public DLIs as an example of an exemption. Confirm on Canada.ca that your exact programme qualifies.

**Does the 150,000 cap mean my application could be refused even with a PAL?**
The cap is a planning target managed mainly through PAL allocations. A PAL secures your place under the cap, but IRCC still assesses your funds, documents and study plan on their own merits.

**Can I count a part-time job towards proof of funds?**
No. Show savings, loans, scholarships or sponsor funds that cover tuition, living costs and travel without relying on future earnings.

**Is $23,448 the final amount?**
That's the figure widely reported from 1 September 2026 for a single applicant outside Quebec. Always check IRCC's proof-of-funds page for the current amount on the day you apply.
