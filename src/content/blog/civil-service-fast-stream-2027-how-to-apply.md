The Civil Service Fast Stream for 2027 entry closes at **midday on 5 November 2026**. As of today, 25 October, you have **11 days** left.

If you're a final-year student, a recent graduate or a master's student who has been thinking about a graduate role in UK government, this is the moment to decide. This guide covers what's confirmed, what you must check on the official site before you start, and a day-by-day plan for the time you have left.

## The confirmed basics

Two independent sources agree on the application window: it opened at **midday on 8 October 2026** and closes at **midday on 5 November 2026**, for entry in 2027. That's what [Newcastle University Careers](https://www.ncl.ac.uk/careers/explore-your-options/occupations/fast-stream/) and [Gorizzume](https://gorizzume.co.uk/graduate-visa-sponsorship/civil-service-fast-stream-2027) both report.

The midday cut-off matters. It isn't 11:59pm. If you're planning to submit on the final day, treat the morning of 5 November as already gone and aim to finish well before.

Other points you can rely on:

- **Where to apply:** through the official [Civil Service Fast Stream site](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/fast-stream) on Civil Service Careers, which links to the application system while the window is open. The [civilservicejobs.app guide](https://civilservicejobs.app/guides/fast-stream) confirms this too. Don't apply through third-party sites.
- **How many stages:** Gorizzume describes a **six-stage process**, which it says it checked against the Fast Stream's own careers pages on 3 October 2026.
- **Annual leave:** Prospects gives a figure of 22 days of annual leave plus 10.5 days of public and other holidays, as quoted in [Debut's 2027 guide](https://debut.careers/civil-service-fast-stream-2027-how-to-apply/). Confirm this in the official offer details.

## What you must check on the official site first

Some important details have changed over recent intakes, and third-party guides don't always keep up. The Fast Stream has also been scaled back in recent years, so older scheme lists may be out of date.

Before you write a single answer, open the official [How to apply page](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/fast-stream/fs-how-to-apply/) and confirm these points:

| Topic | Status | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Deadline (midday 5 Nov 2026) | Confirmed by two sources | Put it in your calendar now |
| Six-stage process | Reported by Gorizzume from official pages | Check what each stage involves and in what order |
| Degree requirements | Check | Confirm the minimum classification and whether final-year students can apply before graduating |
| Nationality and right to work | Check | Essential for international students. Confirm before investing time |
| 2027 salary and location differences | Check | Use the official figure, not older blog posts |
| 2027 scheme list and locations | Check | Older lists may include schemes that no longer run |

| Reasonable adjustments and Disability Confident | Check | Find out how and when to request adjustments |

This takes about 30 minutes, and it will stop you from applying to a scheme that doesn't exist or failing an eligibility rule you didn't know about.

### A note for international students

Several third-party sites, including [Gorizzume](https://gorizzume.co.uk/graduate-visa-sponsorship/civil-service-fast-stream-2027), discuss the Fast Stream and visa sponsorship. Civil Service roles often come with nationality requirements, though, and these can vary between schemes. Read the eligibility and nationality section of the official site before anything else. If the rules aren't clear for your situation, contact the Fast Stream team or your university careers service before the deadline. Don't guess.

## What the job is actually like

It's easy to apply for the Fast Stream because it sounds prestigious and then struggle to explain why you want it. Concrete examples help.

> **Case study: Folashade Atiko.** In [Debut's guide](https://debut.careers/civil-service-fast-stream-2027-how-to-apply/), Fast Streamer Folashade Atiko describes supporting a House of Lords debate and helping to deliver a UK-wide social cohesion strategy.

That gives you a sense of the work: helping to run parts of government that real people depend on, close to ministers and Parliament. If that excites you, say so in your application, and explain why with evidence from your own experience.

Newcastle's careers page also points to official Fast Stream case studies and the Fast Stream blog. Reading two or three of these will give you vocabulary and examples far better than any generic guide.

## Your 11-day plan

You don't need to do everything at once. Here is a realistic plan working back from the deadline.

### Days 1–2 (25–26 October): Check and decide

1. Read the official [Fast Stream site](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/fast-stream) and the [How to apply page](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/fast-stream/fs-how-to-apply/) carefully.
2. Confirm you meet the degree, nationality and right-to-work rules.
3. Read the current scheme list and note which schemes genuinely interest you, and where they're based.
4. Create your account on the official application system so you aren't doing it under pressure later.

### Days 3–5 (27–29 October): Understand the process

1. Find out exactly what the six stages involve and which ones happen before and after the deadline.
2. If there are online tests, look at any official practice material first. Commercial providers such as [JobTestPrep](https://www.jobtestprep.co.uk/free-civil-service-fast-stream-practice) offer overviews and practice tests. They're useful for getting familiar with the format, but treat the official site as the authority on what you'll face.
3. If you need reasonable adjustments, check the official process now and request them early. Don't leave it until the test is open.

### Days 6–8 (30 October – 1 November): Build your evidence

1. List five or six specific examples from your degree, part-time work, volunteering, societies or caring responsibilities.
2. For each one, note the situation, what *you* did, and the result. Government work rewards clear thinking and real outcomes, not big claims.
3. Read two official case studies or blog posts and note what draws you to the work.

### Days 9–10 (2–3 November): Complete and review

1. Fill in every section of the application.
2. Ask someone you trust, or your university careers service, to read anything written.
3. Check your scheme preferences match what you actually want to do.

### Day 11 (4 November): Submit

1. Submit the day **before** the deadline. Websites slow down near closing times, and the cut-off is midday on 5 November, not midnight.
2. Save or screenshot your confirmation.
3. Add any follow-up instructions (test links, deadlines) to your calendar straight away.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Relying on old guides.** Salaries, schemes and stages change between intakes. If a blog post doesn't say "2027", be cautious.
- **Missing the midday cut-off.** Many people assume deadlines run to the end of the day. This one doesn't.
- **Skipping the eligibility check.** This applies especially to international students and anyone who hasn't yet finished their degree.
- **Applying to schemes you can't explain.** Choose schemes you can justify with genuine interest.
- **Ignoring emails after you submit.** Later stages often have their own short deadlines.

## What happens after you submit

The deadline is the start of the process, not the end. The six-stage process carries on after 5 November. The [ProSyllabus 2027 timeline](https://www.prosyllabus.com/hub/civil-service-fast-stream-application-timeline-2027) sets out the stages from the deadline through to assessment. Use it alongside the official site, which should be your final word on dates.

In practice, that means:

- Checking your email (including spam) regularly over the following weeks.
- Keeping some time free in November and beyond in case the next stage opens with a short window.
- Continuing to read official case studies so you're ready to talk about the work in later stages.

### If you're a first- or second-year student

You probably can't apply this round, but you can prepare for the next intake. Bookmark the official site, follow the Fast Stream blog, and look out for events run through university careers services. For example, the University of Portsmouth has hosted a [Fast Stream social mobility network virtual event series](https://myport.port.ac.uk/news-events-and-blogs/events/external-careers-civil-service-fast-stream-fast-stream-social-mobility-network-virtual-event-series). Ask your own careers service what's coming up.

## Quick FAQ

**When exactly does the Fast Stream 2027 close?**
At midday (UK time) on 5 November 2026. The window opened at midday on 8 October 2026.

**Can I apply as an international student?**
It depends on the nationality and right-to-work rules, which can vary by scheme. Check the eligibility section of the [official Fast Stream site](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/fast-stream) before you start.

**What's the 2027 starting salary?**
Third-party guides quote figures, but salaries can change between intakes and may vary by location. Use the figure on the official site.

**How many stages are there?**
Gorizzume reports six stages, based on the Fast Stream's own pages. Check the official site for what each one involves.

**Is it worth buying test-prep packages?**
It's not required. Start with official guidance and free practice material. Commercial practice tests can help you get used to the format, but they aren't official.
