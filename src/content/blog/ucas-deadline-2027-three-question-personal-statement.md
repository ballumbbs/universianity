If you're applying to UK universities for 2027 entry, you have roughly nine weeks until the main UCAS deadline. You probably have less time than that, because your school will want your application before the end of term. Most applicants are still working on the personal statement, which is the part that takes longest.

This is only the second year of the three-question personal statement, and many guides online still describe the old single essay. This article covers the dates you need, how the new format works, and a practical way to write it.

**Who this is for:** UK Year 13 and S6 students, international and EU applicants using UCAS, and the parents and advisers helping them. If you applied to Oxford, Cambridge, or most medicine, dentistry or veterinary courses, your deadline was 15 October 2026, so this guide is less relevant to you.

## The dates that matter

### The main deadline: 13 January 2027, 18:00 UK time

For most undergraduate courses, the deadline is **Wednesday 13 January 2027 at 18:00 UK time**. This is also the deadline for most conservatoire dance, drama and musical theatre courses. Last cycle the date was 14 January 2026, so don't rely on an older calendar.

This date is the **equal consideration** deadline. Universities have to treat every application received by 18:00 that day equally. Sending yours in November gives you no advantage over sending it on 13 January. A careful application in December is better than a rushed one now. For more on what the guarantee covers, see [this explainer on equal consideration](https://smartjamb.com/study/united-kingdom/ucas-equal-consideration-explained-2027/).

**International applicants:** 18:00 UK time could be the middle of the night where you live. Convert it to your local time and give yourself a whole day of margin.

### Your school's deadline comes first

If you apply through a school or college, it will probably set an earlier internal deadline. Staff need time to check your application and add your reference before sending it to UCAS. **Aim to have everything finished by mid-December.** Check the exact date with your sixth form or careers adviser this week.

### The full 2027 timeline

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 September 2026 | Completed applications could be submitted to UCAS from this date |
| 15 October 2026 | Deadline for Oxford, Cambridge and most medicine, dentistry and vet courses (passed) |
| Mid-December 2026 | Typical school internal deadline (check yours) |
| **13 January 2027, 18:00** | **Main equal consideration deadline** |
| 25 February 2027 | UCAS Extra opens (reported by Whatuni) |
| 30 June 2027 | Late application deadline |
| 2 July 2027 | Clearing opens (reported by Whatuni) |
| 23 September 2027 | Final deadline for 2027 entry applications |

One secondary site gives a different final date. UCAS's own [adviser key dates timeline](https://www.ucas.com/advisers/help-and-training/key-dates-timeline) says 23 September 2027, so use that. Dates can change, so always confirm on UCAS's [dates and deadlines page](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/dates-and-deadlines-for-uni-applications).

### Can you apply after 13 January?

Yes, up to 30 June 2027. After that date, you can only apply through Clearing. However, universities don't have to consider late applications equally, and popular courses may already be full. Treat 13 January as your real deadline.

## Fee and choices

- **Fee:** £34.50 for 2027 entry, covering up to **five choices**. Confirm the amount on UCAS's [filling in your application page](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/filling-in-your-ucas-application).
- **Medicine, dentistry and vet:** You can include no more than four of these courses among your five choices.
- **New for 2027, care experience:** If you have care experience, the UCAS form now lets you upload supporting information in the relevant section. You don't have to use it, but it can help universities understand your circumstances and point you to support.

## The three-question personal statement

The personal statement used to be one free-form essay. For 2026 entry, UCAS replaced it with three structured questions, and the same format continues for 2027. The guides we checked all describe the format in the same way. Your UCAS Hub is the final authority, though, so **check the exact wording and limits on the form itself** before you finalise anything.

### The three questions

1. **Why do you want to study this course or subject?**
2. **How have your qualifications and studies helped you to prepare for this course or subject?**
3. **A third question about what you've done outside education**, and why those experiences are useful preparation. Copy the exact wording from your UCAS Hub and answer that wording, not a paraphrase.

### The length limits

Guides consistently report these limits:

- **4,000 characters in total, including spaces**, across all three answers.
- **At least 350 characters per answer.**
- **No fourth question.** All your content has to fit within these three.

4,000 characters is shorter than most people expect, so count characters, not words. Paste your drafts into the form, or into a counter, often.

### How to split your 4,000 characters

UCAS doesn't set a split beyond the minimum. One widely shared rule of thumb is below. Use it as a starting point, not a requirement.

| Question | Suggested range | What it's doing |
|---|---|---|
| Q1: Why this subject | 900–1,200 | Motivation and genuine interest |
| Q2: Studies and qualifications | 1,500–2,000 | Academic evidence: usually your strongest material |
| Q3: Outside education | 700–1,000 | Wider experiences and what they taught you |

If your best evidence comes from a job, caring responsibilities or independent projects, give Q3 more space. Spend your characters where your strongest material is.

## How to write each answer

### Question 1: Why this course?

Admissions tutors want to see real, specific interest. Avoid opening lines like "I have always been passionate about…".

- **Start with a specific idea, problem or question** that drew you to the subject, then explain why it still interests you.
- **Show you understand what the degree involves.** Applying for economics is not the same as liking the news. Mention an area of the subject you want to study further.
- **Write for all five choices.** You submit one statement to every university. Don't name a university, and if your choices cover related subjects, focus on what they have in common.

### Question 2: How have your studies prepared you?

This is where you give academic evidence. You don't need to list your grades, because they already appear elsewhere on the form.

- **Choose two or three moments from your courses:** a topic, a project, an extended essay or a coursework piece. For each one, explain what you did and what you learned.
- **Connect each example to the degree.** For example: "In A level Chemistry I found X, which is why I'm interested in Y at university."
- **Include reading or study beyond the syllabus** if it grew out of your schoolwork: a book, a lecture, an online course or a competition. Say what you thought about it, not just that you did it.

### Question 3: What else have you done?

The second half of this question matters most: *why* the experiences are useful.

- **Use any relevant experience:** part-time work, volunteering, caring for a family member, clubs, personal projects, travel or independent research.
- **For each one, name a skill or insight and connect it to your course.** "I worked in a café" says little. "Managing weekend rushes taught me to prioritise under pressure, which will help with lab deadlines" makes the connection clear.
- **Choose a few experiences and explain them well.** With about 1,000 characters, two well-explained experiences are better than six in a list.

## A five-week writing plan

If your school deadline is mid-December, you can follow this plan:

1. **Week 1: Gather material.** Note every relevant experience, book, project and moment. Don't write full sentences yet.
2. **Week 2: Write rough drafts of all three answers.** Go over the limit at this stage; you'll cut later.
3. **Week 3: Cut and connect.** Get the total under 4,000 characters, check each answer has at least 350, and make sure every example connects to the subject.
4. **Week 4: Get feedback.** Ask a subject teacher to check the content, and ask someone outside the subject whether it's clear.
5. **Week 5: Final check.** Paste the answers into the UCAS form, check the character counts there, read everything aloud and correct any errors.

Write in your own words throughout. Admissions tutors read thousands of statements and can tell when one is generic or doesn't sound like the applicant.

## Quick FAQ

**Is there any advantage to submitting before 13 January?**
Not for equal consideration. Every application received by 18:00 UK time on 13 January 2027 must be treated equally. Your school's internal deadline is the one to plan around.

**Can I write a different statement for each university?**
No. All five choices receive the same three answers, so don't name a specific university.

**What if I don't have much to write for Question 3?**
Household responsibilities, part-time jobs, independent reading and personal projects all count. What matters is that you explain why each experience is useful for your course. Make sure each answer meets the minimum length.

**Do these guides apply if I'm an international student?**
Yes. The same form, questions and deadlines apply. Convert 18:00 UK time to your local time zone, and allow time for your referee to complete their part.

**What happens if I miss 13 January?**
You can still apply until 30 June 2027, but universities don't have to consider your application equally, and some courses may be full. After 30 June, Clearing is the main route.
