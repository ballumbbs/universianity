The 2027–28 Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) is open now. The U.S. Department of Education launched it on **Wednesday, 23 September 2026**, about a week before the 1 October date set in law. This is the second year in a row it has opened early. The 2026–27 form opened on 24 September 2025. You can confirm the launch on the official [FSA Partners updates page](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/fafsacentral/Updates) and in the Department's [press release](https://www.ed.gov/about/news/press-release/us-department-of-education-announces-earliest-fafsa-launch-program-history-second-consecutive-year).

This guide covers what changed, the new Pell Grant rules, and the step-by-step process. It focuses most on the deadlines that decide how much aid you actually get.

## Who needs to file

- **High school seniors** starting college or career school in autumn 2027.
- **Current college students.** The FAFSA has to be filed again every year.
- **Anyone attending school between 1 July 2027 and 30 June 2028.**
- **International families with a US-citizen or eligible non-citizen child.** A new foreign-income rule may affect Pell eligibility (see below).

If your family already submitted the form during the summer beta testing period, that counts as a real submission. **Do not file again.** The Department's [early access notice](https://awsfinancialaidtoolkit.ed.gov/tk/announcement-detail.jsp?id=fafsa-updates) explains how the beta worked.

## Why the 30 June 2028 deadline is misleading

Officially, the 2027–28 FAFSA closes on **30 June 2028**. That date matters very little for planning. Three other kinds of deadline decide how much money you receive:

| Deadline type | Typical timing | What it controls |
|---|---|---|
| College priority deadlines | Often November–December 2026 at many private colleges | Institutional grants, scholarships, work-study |
| State priority deadlines | Often January–March 2027 | State grants. Some are first come, first served |
| CSS Profile (some colleges) | Set by each college, often alongside its FAFSA date | Institutional aid at colleges that require it |
| Federal deadline | 30 June 2028 | Last chance for federal aid such as Pell and federal loans |

Every college sets its own priority date, and many private colleges also require the College Board's CSS Profile in addition to the FAFSA. Some states hand out aid until the money runs out, so filing late can mean getting nothing even if you qualify. **Check each college's financial aid page and your state's higher education agency for exact dates.** They vary widely and can change from year to year.

## What's new on the 2027–28 form

The changes are mostly about making the form easier to use. Based on the Department's announcement and [CUNY's summary](https://www.cuny.edu/financial-aid/fafsa/):

- **QR-code invitations.** Students can scan a QR code to send parents or a spouse a customised text message inviting them to join the form. Email invitations still work.
- **Clearer student questions.** The wording of the questions about college and career plans, homelessness and high school completion has been revised based on user research.
- **"Review & Sign."** The signature section has a new name, and signing now happens on a single page.
- **"Background."** The old "Personal Circumstances" section has been renamed.
- **Territory tax returns.** Each contributor is asked whether they filed a 2025 tax return in Puerto Rico or another US territory.
- **Faster results.** Your FAFSA Submission Summary, including your Student Aid Index (SAI), is usually ready as soon as you submit. Some forms still take one to three days. The Department says the form can take as little as 15 minutes.

The final 2027–28 PDF form and Submission Summary were published on 6 October 2026 in announcement [APP-26-14](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2026-10-06/final-2027-28-fafsa-pdf-form-fafsa-form-incarcerated-applicants-and-fafsa-submission-summary). It is worth skimming if you want to see every question in advance.

## Before you start: gather these

1. **A StudentAid.gov account (FSA ID) for the student and for each contributor.** Contributors are the parents or spouse whose information the form needs. Each person needs their own account, and new accounts can take a little time to verify, so set them up early.
2. **2025 tax returns and W-2s.** The 2027–28 form uses "prior-prior year" income, which means 2025 data.
3. **Current bank balances and investment values,** as of the day you file.
4. **A photo ID.** Keep it nearby in case you are asked to confirm your identity. Check StudentAid.gov for current identity-verification steps.
5. **Your list of colleges.** Add every school you're applying to, even ones you're unsure about. Schools you leave off won't receive your information.

## Filling it in, section by section

### Student section
You start the form, answer the dependency and Background questions, and invite contributors by QR code or email. Most undergraduates under 24 are treated as dependent students and need a parent contributor.

### Which parent files
If your parents are divorced or separated, the contributor is **the parent who provides more financial support**. This is not necessarily the parent you live with.

### Financial information
Contributors give consent for the IRS to transfer federal tax data directly into the form. Then you answer the asset questions.

**Don't report what the formula doesn't ask for.** A common mistake is including items that are excluded, which can make your SAI higher than it should be:

- **Retirement accounts** (401(k)s, IRAs and similar): not reported.
- **Home equity in your main home:** not reported.
- **Family-owned small businesses** with fewer than 100 full-time (or full-time-equivalent) employees: excluded from net worth under the new law.
- **Family farms the family lives on:** excluded from net worth.

[NYIT's summary](https://www.nyit.edu/admissions/costs-and-aid/financial-aid/how-to-apply/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-fsa-changes/) lists the business and farm exclusions.

### Review & Sign
Check your list of schools and every figure, then sign on the single signing page. Each contributor signs their own section. The form isn't complete until everyone has signed.

## The new Pell Grant rules (OBBBA)

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1) was signed on 4 July 2025. Most of its financial aid changes took effect on 1 July 2026, so they apply fully to 2027–28. Three changes affect Pell Grants:

### 1. An SAI cutoff
Students with an SAI greater than **twice the maximum Pell Grant** are no longer eligible for Pell. The maximum Pell was $7,395 for 2026–27, which put the cutoff at $14,790, as [UIC explains](https://financialaid.uic.edu/aid-process/one-big-beautiful-bill/). **The 2027–28 maximum may differ, so check StudentAid.gov's [Pell Grant page](https://studentaid.gov/articles/dont-miss-out-on-pell-grants/) for the current figure.**

### 2. The full-coverage rule
If your grants and scholarships from other sources already cover your full Cost of Attendance, you can't also receive Pell.

### 3. Foreign income counts
Foreign income is now included in the adjusted gross income (AGI) used to calculate Pell eligibility. This matters most for families who earn abroad or move between countries. [NASFAA's brief](https://www.nasfaa.org/uploads/documents/OB3_Leadership_Brief_Pell.pdf) explains the details.

## After you submit

1. **Read your FAFSA Submission Summary.** It shows your SAI and flags anything that needs attention.
2. **Fix mistakes quickly.** You can log in to StudentAid.gov and make corrections, including adding schools.
3. **Respond to college requests.** Colleges may ask for verification documents. Late replies can delay your aid offer.
4. **Ask for a special circumstances review if things have changed.** The form uses 2025 income. If your family's finances have worsened since then, for example through job loss, medical bills or divorce, file normally first. Then contact each college's financial aid office and ask for a professional judgement review. This has to be done school by school.

## Deadline checklist

- [ ] Create FSA IDs for the student and every contributor, this week.
- [ ] Write down each college's priority aid deadline. Many fall in November–December 2026.
- [ ] Check whether each college requires the CSS Profile, and note its deadline.
- [ ] Look up your state's grant deadline. Many fall in January–March 2027, and some are first come, first served.
- [ ] Submit the FAFSA before the earliest of these dates.
- [ ] Review your Submission Summary and correct any errors.

## Quick FAQ

**Is it too early to file in October or November?**
No. The form is open, and filing early is the best way to meet college and state priority deadlines.

**We did the summer beta. Do we file again?**
No. Beta submissions are real submissions.

**Our income dropped after 2025. Will the FAFSA reflect that?**
Not automatically. File with your 2025 data, then ask each college's aid office for a special circumstances review.

**Do I report my parents' retirement savings or house?**
No. Retirement accounts and the family home aren't reported. Qualifying small family businesses and family farms you live on are also excluded.

**I'm a US citizen living abroad. Does anything change?**
Foreign income now counts in the AGI used for Pell eligibility, so your Pell result may differ from earlier years. Speak to each college's aid office if you're unsure.
