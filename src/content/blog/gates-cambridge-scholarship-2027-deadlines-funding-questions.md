The Gates Cambridge international round for October 2027 entry is open. Depending on your course, your funding deadline is either **Tuesday 8 December 2026** or **Wednesday 6 January 2027**, which leaves you somewhere between three and eight weeks. One related date is even closer: the Cambridge application fee-waiver cut-off on **24 November 2026**.

This guide covers which deadline applies to you, how the application works, what the scholarship pays for, and how to answer the Gates funding questions well.

## The short version

- **What it is:** A full-cost scholarship for citizens of any country outside the UK to study a PhD, MLitt, or a one-year full-time postgraduate course such as an MPhil or LLM at the University of Cambridge.
- **How you apply:** Through your normal Cambridge course application. You answer the Gates questions in its funding section. There is no separate Gates form.
- **Deadline:** 8 December 2026 or 6 January 2027, depending on your course. Check your course page.
- **Who it's for now:** International applicants, including US citizens who live outside the US.

## Step 1: Find your real deadline

The most common mistake is to assume you have until 6 January. Cambridge's [postgraduate funding page](https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/funding) says the funding deadline depends on your course and tells you to check the course page. For 2027/28 entry there are two main University funding deadlines:

| Funding deadline | Who it applies to | Fee-waiver request deadline (if eligible) |
|---|---|---|
| Tuesday 8 December 2026 | Courses listed with the December funding deadline | 24 November 2026 |
| Wednesday 6 January 2027 | Courses listed with the January funding deadline | 11 December 2026 |

If your course page shows 8 December, that is your date. Anyone applying for University funding must apply by the 8 December funding deadline when it applies to their course. Many master's courses fall on the December date. [GradPilot's course-by-course roundup](https://gradpilot.com/news/oxford-cambridge-masters-deadlines-2027) gives Cambridge examples.

**What to do today:**

1. Open your course in the Cambridge Postgraduate Course Directory.
2. Write down the funding deadline shown there, not the general course deadline.
3. Put it in your calendar a week early. Portals slow down near deadlines, and referees need time.

### Which round are you in?

- **US citizens living in the USA:** That round has already closed. One guide puts the deadline at 14 October 2026, 11:59pm UK time.
- **US citizens studying or working outside the US:** You apply in the **international round**, so the December and January deadlines apply to you.
- **Everyone else outside the UK:** International round.
- **UK citizens:** Not eligible for Gates Cambridge, though other Cambridge funding exists. The fee-waiver section below is still relevant to you.

Applying in the wrong round is an avoidable mistake. If you're unsure, check the official [How to Apply page](https://www.gatescambridge.org/apply/how-to-apply/).

## Step 2: Check the fee-waiver cut-off (24 November)

Cambridge charges an application fee. Some applicants can ask for it to be waived, but the request deadline comes well before the funding deadline. According to the [official fee-waiver page](https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/apply/how/application-fee-waiver), if you are applying for funding you must submit your request by:

- **24 November 2026** for courses with the 8 December 2026 funding deadline
- **11 December 2026** for courses with the 6 January 2027 funding deadline

The waiver is aimed at **UK applicants and applicants with refugee, asylum or humanitarian status**. Most international Gates applicants won't qualify. If you have refugee or similar status, though, this is the date to act on first. Read the eligibility rules on the official page rather than relying on a summary.

## Step 3: Understand how the application works

Gates Cambridge is not a separate scheme you apply to. The process looks like this:

1. **Apply for your Cambridge course** (PhD, MLitt, MPhil, LLM or another eligible one-year full-time course) through the University's application portal.
2. **In the funding section, indicate you want to be considered for Gates Cambridge** and answer the Gates questions there.
3. **Tick every other funding option that fits you.** The same funding section is used to consider applicants for Cambridge Trust and other University funding, so don't leave boxes unticked out of modesty.
4. **Make sure your references arrive on time.** Your referees support both the academic application and the funding case.

The [official How to Apply page](https://www.gatescambridge.org/apply/how-to-apply/) lists the funding questions with their exact word and character limits. Third-party guides describe four questions. Read the official wording yourself before you draft, because limits are enforced in characters and small changes do happen between cycles.

## Step 4: Write funding answers that meet the four criteria

Gates Cambridge selects on four official criteria:

1. **Intellectual ability**
2. **Justification for your choice of course**
3. **A commitment to improving the lives of others**
4. **Leadership capacity**

Your academic record, references and research proposal (for PhDs) mostly show the first two. The funding questions are where you show the last two.

### The "improving lives" question

The official wording asks you to give an example or examples of how you have shown a commitment to improving the lives of others, why this matters to you, and how it relates to your future plans. The limit is about 300 words, up to 2,100 characters.

That is a small space, so:

- **Pick one or two examples, not five.** Depth matters more than a list.
- **Show what changed.** Who benefited, how many people, and what was different afterwards? Use numbers you can stand behind.
- **Answer the "why".** One honest sentence about your motivation is worth more than a paragraph of general statements.
- **Connect it to Cambridge.** Show how this course is the next step toward the impact you describe.

### The leadership question

This question asks for an example or examples of how you've shown a capacity for leadership, "by initiating a change or bringing others together to achieve a goal."

Leadership here doesn't have to mean holding a title. Strong examples often look like this:

- You noticed a problem nobody owned and fixed it.
- You got people who disagreed to work together.
- You started something (a project, a lab protocol, a community programme) that kept going after you left.

Structure each example as **situation → what you did → result**, and keep the focus on your own actions.

### The other questions

Make sure your answers to the remaining questions shown on the official page fit with your course choice and future plans. Panels read the whole application together, so contradictions stand out.

## Step 5: Brief your referees

Referees who only write "excellent student, top 5%" don't give the panel much to work with. Send each referee a short note (half a page is enough) covering:

- The course you're applying for and why
- The four Gates selection criteria
- One or two specific moments they saw that show leadership or a commitment to others
- Your funding deadline, with a reminder date a week before it

The [University of Michigan fellowships office summary](https://lsa.umich.edu/onsf/scholarships/future-graduate-program-career-experience/gates-cambridge-scholarship.html) is a useful second explanation of the criteria that you can share with referees.

## What the scholarship covers

According to the official [Current Scholars page](https://www.gatescambridge.org/current-scholars/) and guides that cite it, a Gates Cambridge Scholarship covers:

- **The University Composition Fee** (tuition)
- **A maintenance allowance of £23,152 for 12 months** at the 2026–27 rate, pro rata for courses shorter than 12 months
- **One economy single airfare** at both the start and end of the course, or a return ticket for courses shorter than 10 months
- **Inbound visa costs** and the **Immigration Health Surcharge**

The allowance is paid during the first week of each term. The first term's payment is reduced by any advance funding you receive.

You may see £21,000 or £22,050 on other sites. Those figures are out of date. The 2027–28 rate may be higher than £23,152, so check the official page when you're offered a place.

## How competitive is it, and what happens next?

Third-party guides estimate **around 70–80 scholarships a year** across both rounds, with roughly 55 in the international round. These numbers are approximate and not confirmed officially, but they show that this is one of the most selective awards in the world.

One tracker, [Borderless](https://borderless.so/scholarships/cambridge/gates-cambridge-scholarship), gives this international-round timeline:

- Applications opened: 11 September 2026
- Panel review and selection: around 22 January 2027
- Offers: February 2027

Shortlisted candidates are often interviewed by video, but the details for this cycle aren't confirmed. Watch your email from January onwards and check the official site.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming the deadline is 6 January** when your course says 8 December.
- **Missing the fee-waiver cut-off** if you're eligible.
- **Applying in the wrong round.** US citizens living abroad belong in the international round.
- **Generic answers.** "I am passionate about helping people" without evidence won't get you far.
- **Unbriefed referees** who don't know you're applying for Gates.
- **Unticked funding boxes** that cost you consideration for other Cambridge awards.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I need a separate Gates Cambridge application?**
No. You answer the Gates questions in the funding section of your Cambridge course application.

**I'm a US citizen working in Europe. Did I miss the deadline?**
Probably not. US citizens living outside the US apply in the international round, which closes on 8 December 2026 or 6 January 2027 depending on the course.

**Can I apply for an MPhil or only a PhD?**
PhD, MLitt and one-year full-time postgraduate courses such as an MPhil or LLM are all eligible.

**How much is the stipend?**
£23,152 for 12 months at the 2026–27 rate, plus tuition, flights, visa costs and the Immigration Health Surcharge. Check the official page for the 2027–28 figure.

**When will I hear back?**
One tracker says panels meet around late January 2027 and offers go out in February 2027. Treat these dates as approximate.
