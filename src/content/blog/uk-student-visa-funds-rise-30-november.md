If you're heading to a UK university in January 2027, or planning ahead for September 2027, the amount of money you need to show for your Student visa is going up. The new rates start on **30 November 2026**, which is only weeks away.

This guide covers what's changing, how much you'll need, and how to time your application and bank balance so a technicality doesn't sink your visa.

> **Before you act:** the figures here come from published news reports and immigration advisers. They agree with each other, but the Home Office's own guidance is the final word. Check the GOV.UK Student visa "money" page and the September 2026 Statement of Changes to the Immigration Rules before you move any funds.

## What's changing

The UK Home Office announced the change in an update published on **3 September 2026**, according to [The CSR Journal](https://thecsrjournal.in/uk-student-visa-maintenance-fund-requirements-to-rise-in-2026). The rules were part of the Statement of Changes to the Immigration Rules known as HC 584. Summaries are available from [UKCISA](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/news/student-update-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-september-2026/) and [DavidsonMorris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-584-3-september-2026/).

From **30 November 2026**, Student visa applicants will need to show:

- **£1,570 a month** if studying in London (up from £1,529)
- **£1,203 a month** if studying outside London

You show these amounts for up to **nine months**. For courses of nine months or longer, the maximum is nine months' worth of living costs. Immigration law firm [Morgan Smith Immigration](https://morgansmithimmigration.com/uk-student-visa-maintenance-requirement-increase-november-2026/) and [UK Visa Expert](https://ukvisaexpert.com/16364-2/) both confirm the start date and the nine-month cap.

For a London student on a course of nine months or more, that's **£369 more** to show than under the current rate. It isn't huge, but if your family has budgeted to the exact pound, being £369 short is enough for a refusal.

## Living costs aren't the whole bill

The monthly figure covers **living costs only**. Under the long-standing rules, you must also show any **course fees you still owe** for your first year (or the whole course if it's shorter than a year). Your CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) should state your fees and any amount you've already paid.

Here's a hypothetical example:

- London course, more than nine months long
- First-year fees on the CAS: £20,000
- Deposit already paid (and shown on the CAS): £5,000
- Fees still owed: £15,000
- Living costs from 30 November: £14,130
- **Total to show: £29,130**

If your university classes your course as London-based, use the London rate. The definition of "London" is set out in the Home Office guidance. It's based on where you study, not where you live. If you're unsure, ask your university's international office which rate applies to your CAS.

## The two key rules: how long and how recent

These long-standing requirements trip up more applicants than the amount itself. Confirm the current wording on GOV.UK.

1. **The 28-day rule.** The full amount must be in the account for **28 consecutive days**. If the balance drops below the required figure for even one day, the 28 days start again.
2. **The 31-day rule.** The last day of that 28-day period (the closing date on your bank statement) must be **no more than 31 days before** you submit your application.

In practice, you need the right amount in the bank, kept untouched, for a full four weeks, and then you must apply soon afterwards.

## When should you apply?

Advisers covering this change say the higher amounts apply to applications made from 30 November 2026. That gives you two routes. Check the official guidance to confirm how the date of application is treated.

### Option A: Apply before 30 November

If you already have your CAS and your funds have been in the account for 28 days, applying before 30 November means you should be assessed at the current rates.

This works best if:

- Your CAS has been issued
- Your funds already meet the **current** requirement and have done for 28 days
- You can book biometrics and gather documents in time

Don't rush a messy application just to beat the deadline. A refused application costs you the visa fee and time. Being assessed at slightly lower rates isn't worth that risk.

### Option B: Apply on or after 30 November

If your CAS isn't ready yet, or you're applying for September 2027, plan around the **new** figures from the start.

This matters because of the 28-day rule. If you plan to apply in early December, your account must already hold the higher amount by early November. Topping up the week before you apply won't work.

### Quick timeline

| You plan to submit | Higher balance must be in place by (roughly) |
|---|---|
| Early December 2026 | Early November 2026 |
| Mid-December 2026 | Mid-November 2026 |
| January 2027 | December 2026 |

These are approximate. Count 28 full days back from your statement's closing date, and make sure you apply within 31 days of that date.

## Step-by-step: getting your funds right

1. **Get your CAS details.** Note the course location (London or not), the length, the fees and any deposit already recorded.
2. **Calculate your total.** Monthly rate × number of months (maximum nine), plus outstanding fees. Use the **new** rate unless you're sure you'll apply before 30 November.
3. **Add a buffer.** Exchange rates move. If the money is held in naira, rupees or another currency, keep extra above the minimum so a currency dip doesn't push you below it.
4. **Choose whose account.** Funds can usually be in your own name or a parent's or legal guardian's. Parental accounts need extra documents, such as proof of relationship and a consent letter. Check GOV.UK for the exact list.
5. **Deposit and leave it alone.** Once the full amount is in, don't withdraw anything, not even a small transfer, for 28 days.
6. **Get the right statement.** It should show the account holder's name, the bank's name and the balance across the full period, and it should be dated correctly.
7. **Apply within 31 days** of the statement's closing date.

## Who's exempt?

Under the existing rules, you generally don't need to show living-cost funds if you've been living in the UK with permission for **12 months or more** on the date you apply. For example, this can apply if you're switching or extending your visa from inside the UK. Confirm on GOV.UK whether this applies to you before you rely on it.

## A note for parents

If you're the one holding the money, the biggest risk is cash flow. A school fee payment, a business expense or a family emergency that briefly drops your balance below the threshold can restart the 28-day clock. If possible, put the funds in a separate account that isn't used for day-to-day spending until the visa is granted.

## Quick FAQ

**How much do I need for a London course from 30 November 2026?**
£1,570 a month for up to nine months, so £14,130 for a course of nine months or longer, plus any course fees you still owe.

**And outside London?**
£1,203 a month, so £10,827 for nine months, plus outstanding fees. Check GOV.UK to confirm.

**I'm starting in January 2027. Am I affected?**
Yes, if you apply on or after 30 November 2026. Most January applicants will apply around then, so check your timing now. [UK Visa Expert](https://ukvisaexpert.com/16364-2/) flags the January 2027 intake as the group most immediately affected.

**Can I top up the money right before I apply?**
No. The full amount must be held for 28 consecutive days, so top up early.

**Do I still need to show funds if I've paid all my fees?**
You still need living costs unless you're exempt. Fees you've already paid (as recorded on your CAS) are subtracted from the fee portion.
