The European Commission has published the dates for the next round of its Blue Book traineeship. This is the paid, five-month programme that places graduates in Commission departments and other EU bodies, mainly in Brussels and Luxembourg. Applications for the **October 2027 session open on 15 February 2027**, which leaves about three months to get ready.

That time matters. The application isn't hard, but it asks for documents and language evidence that take a while to gather. Some guides online are also out of date. One respected explainer written in September 2026 said the window hadn't been announced yet, and at least one listing shows a wrong deadline. This guide uses the [official calendar of sessions](https://traineeships.ec.europa.eu/about/calendar-sessions_en) as its source for dates.

## Key dates at a glance

| Stage | October 2027 session | March 2027 session (for reference) |
|---|---|---|
| Applications open | 15 February 2027, 10:00 (Brussels time) | 22 July 2026, 10:00 (Brussels time) |
| Applications close | 12 March 2027, 10:00 (Brussels time) | 4 September 2026 |
| Pre-selection | Expected around March–April 2027 | September–October 2026 |
| Recruitment / offers | Expected around June–July 2027 | November–December 2026 |
| Traineeship runs | 1 October 2027 – 29 February 2028 | 1 March – 31 July 2027 |

The "expected" rows are estimates. They're based on how previous sessions ran: the October 2026 session had applications in March–April, pre-selection in April–May and recruitment in June–July. Check the official pages for confirmed stage dates.

**A warning about the deadline.** Some third-party listings give a closing date of 7 April 2027 and an end date of 28 February 2028. Those figures appear to be carried over from an older timetable. The official calendar says applications close on **12 March 2027 at 10:00 Brussels time**. Note that this is a morning deadline, not midnight. 2028 is a leap year, so the traineeship really does end on 29 February.

## What the traineeship is

The Blue Book programme runs twice a year, with sessions starting in March and October. According to the [eutesting.org explainer](https://eutesting.org/guide/blue-book-traineeship.html), there are around 1,000 paid places per session. Trainees work inside Commission departments and some other EU bodies on real policy, legal, communications or administrative tasks.

There are two tracks:

- **Administrative traineeships.** There are two routes:
  - **ADMIN UG** for university graduates.
  - **ADMIN VET** for graduates of vocational education and training. This route is new.
- **Translation traineeships (DGT)** with the Directorate-General for Translation.

You can submit **only one application per session and per type of traineeship**. Decide which track suits you before you start.

## Eligibility checklist

The points below come from secondary summaries of the official rules. Read the eligibility page on traineeships.ec.europa.eu before you apply, because details can change between sessions.

### Education

- **ADMIN UG:** you need a completed higher education degree, described as a standard three-year degree.
- **ADMIN VET:** for the first time, graduates of vocational education and training can apply. The [EU Neighbours East announcement](https://euneighbourseast.eu/opportunities/become-a-european-commission-trainee-applications-are-now-open/) says that if you didn't go to university, you may still be eligible.
- **No age limit.** The programme isn't restricted to people in their early twenties.

### Languages (EU nationals)

- **English, French or German at C1 or C2.**
- **One other official EU language at B2 or above.**
- A native language counts for either requirement.

In practice, a German graduate with fluent English already meets both requirements. An Irish graduate with only English would need a second EU language at B2.

### Previous EU experience: the six-week rule

You can't have spent more than **6 weeks (42 calendar days)** working or training in any EU institution, body or agency. That includes earlier traineeships and short contracts. If you've done a stint inside an EU body, count the days carefully.

### Non-EU nationals

Graduates from outside the EU can apply. Sources disagree on the details, though:

- Some summaries, such as [World Funding Info](https://worldfundinginfo.com/european-commission-blue-book-traineeship-programme-2027/), say opportunities for non-EU nationals exist in specific departments.
- [Scholarships.af](https://scholarships.af/opportunity/eu-blue-book-traineeship/) says non-EU places are limited to candidate (pre-accession) countries.

**Check the official FAQ and eligibility page** to see which rules apply to your nationality. Also check which language requirements apply to you, as they may differ from those for EU nationals. Don't rely on any third-party summary for this point, including this one.

## How selection works

The process has more stages than a normal internship application.

1. **You apply online** using an EU Login account. You fill in the form and upload supporting documents.
2. **Pre-selection.** Assessors review eligible applications. The strongest candidates go into the **Virtual Blue Book**, a database of pre-selected candidates.
3. **Recruitment.** Commission departments search the Virtual Blue Book and choose the trainees they want. Only then do you receive an offer.

Getting into the Virtual Blue Book **does not guarantee a place**. The eutesting.org guide estimates there are around three times more candidates in the Blue Book than places available. That's why the quality of your written answers matters. Departments are searching for profiles that match their work.

An application is valid for one session only. If you're unsuccessful, you can apply again in a later session.

## The grant and money

Trainees receive a monthly grant to cover living costs. For the March 2027 session, the figure quoted was **€1,538**. The official FAQ has an indicative grants table, and amounts vary by location (Brussels or Luxembourg). **Check the FAQ for the October 2027 figure** before you build a budget around it.

You must **find accommodation yourself**. The Commission doesn't house trainees. Brussels and Luxembourg are expensive, especially Luxembourg. In practice:

- Budget for a deposit and first month's rent before your first grant arrives.
- Look at shared flats and short-let rooms. Many trainees only need a five-month lease.
- Start searching as soon as you get an offer. Each session starts with roughly a thousand people looking for housing in the same cities at the same time.

## What to do before 15 February

The application window is less than four weeks long. Do the slow tasks now.

1. **Create your EU Login account.** It's free, and you can set it up in advance. That way you won't be fixing password or verification problems on deadline day.
2. **Gather your diploma documents.** Scan your degree or VET certificate clearly. If you're graduating soon, check the official rules on whether your degree must be finished by the application deadline.
3. **Get evidence of your language levels.** That could be language certificates, a degree taught in that language, or other proof the form accepts. If you're relying on a B2 in a second language, make sure you can actually show it.
4. **Count your EU days.** If you've been inside any EU institution, body or agency before, list the exact dates to check you're under 42 days.
5. **Choose your track.** Pick ADMIN UG, ADMIN VET or DGT. You can't submit two applications to the same type in one session.
6. **Draft your written answers offline.** Write your motivation and your description of your specific knowledge and experience in a document first. Be concrete:
   - courses you've taken
   - theses or projects you've completed
   - policy areas you know well
   - tools and languages you use

   Remember that departments search the Virtual Blue Book, so clear, specific descriptions of your skills make you easier to find.
7. **Set a reminder for 12 March 2027, 09:00 Brussels time.** Then submit days earlier anyway.

## If you applied for March 2027

If you applied in the July–September 2026 window, you're now in the stage where trainees are recruited from the Virtual Blue Book. Under the published timetable, that runs around November–December. If you don't receive an offer, it isn't a rejection of your profile. Many qualified candidates aren't picked simply because there are fewer places. You can apply again for October 2027, and you can use what you learned to sharpen your written answers.

## Quick FAQ

**When is the deadline for the October 2027 Blue Book traineeship?**
12 March 2027 at 10:00 a.m. Brussels time, according to the official calendar. Ignore listings that say 7 April.

**Is the Blue Book traineeship paid?**
Yes. Trainees get a monthly grant, quoted at €1,538 for the March 2027 session. The amount depends on location, so check the official FAQ for the current figure.

**Can I apply without a university degree?**
Possibly. The new ADMIN VET route is open to graduates of vocational education and training. Check the official eligibility rules for the qualifications that count.

**I'm not an EU citizen. Can I apply?**
Non-EU graduates can apply, but sources disagree on which departments and nationalities are covered. Confirm your eligibility in the official FAQ before you invest time in an application.

**Does getting into the Virtual Blue Book mean I'm accepted?**
No. It means you've been pre-selected. Departments then choose trainees from the database, and there are about three candidates for every place.
