If you got your first MenB jab before or during Freshers 2026, the job isn't finished. The free NHS catch-up programme for new university students is a **two-dose course**, and one dose on its own doesn't give the protection the programme is designed to provide.

This guide covers what is confirmed, what to check before you book, and how to get dose two without fuss. Some details, such as exact dose gaps and deadlines, are set by the NHS and can change, so the official pages are linked throughout.

## Why there's a MenB catch-up at all

Meningitis B is a bacterial infection that can cause meningitis and sepsis. Starting university is one of the higher-risk times: lots of people from different places mixing closely in halls, at events and on nights out.

Babies in the UK have been offered MenB through the routine infant programme since 2015, so most current students were born too early to get it. In 2026 the NHS ran what Medscape described as its "biggest ever catch-up campaign" against MenB. According to [Medscape's report from 3 August 2026](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/nearly-100-000-young-people-get-menb-vaccine-fortnight-2026a1000qdv), nearly 100,000 young people had their first dose in under two weeks.

That means a lot of first-years are now due their second dose as winter starts.

## Are you eligible?

The Department for Education promoted the campaign as "get your MenB vaccines before Freshers 2026". Its [vaccines for students guide](https://educationhub.blog.gov.uk/2026/02/vaccines-for-students-how-to-get-up-to-date/) lists two groups:

- **Anyone born between 1 September 2007 and 31 August 2008**, which covers most people who finished Year 13 in summer 2026.
- **Anyone born on or after 21 July 2001 who is starting university for the first time**, including international students.

The UK Health Security Agency has a dedicated explainer, [Who is eligible for the 2026 one-off MenB vaccine programme?](https://ukhsa.blog.gov.uk/2026/06/12/who-is-eligible-for-the-new-one-off-menb-vaccine-programme/). Check the full wording there if your situation is borderline.

### Who it's usually not for

- **Postgraduates.** [Queen Mary University of London](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/student-health/vaccinations/meningitis-information/) says the 2026–27 programme is aimed at first-time university starters, which "typically does not include postgraduate students".
- **Returning students** in second year or above, who aren't first-time starters.
- **People vaccinated as babies.** [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/meningitis-b-vaccine-rollout-begins-for-students-what-you-need-to-know-287448) notes that anyone who had MenB through the infant programme introduced in 2015 should already be protected and isn't the main target.

If you're not eligible for the free programme, it's still worth reading the MenACWY section below. That vaccine has its own free offer.

## How to get your second dose: step by step

1. **Find your dose-one record.** Look for the date, the place, and any card or text confirmation. If you had it at a pharmacy, they will have a record. If you're registered with a GP in England, it may also show in the NHS App.
2. **Check the timing.** There's a minimum gap between the two doses. Ask the pharmacist or nurse who gave dose one, or check the [UKHSA eligibility page](https://ukhsa.blog.gov.uk/2026/06/12/who-is-eligible-for-the-new-one-off-menb-vaccine-programme/). Don't guess, because a dose given too early may not count.
3. **Check the programme is still running.** This is a one-off catch-up, not a permanent offer. Confirm on the official page that you can still get dose two free, and book soon rather than leaving it until the end of term.
4. **Choose where to go.** The Conversation reports the jabs are free at pharmacies for students starting university this autumn. If you're near campus, ask your university health centre or local GP too, since some may offer it.
5. **Book or call ahead.** Ring or book online with a pharmacy near your university. Tell them you're a first-year student on the MenB catch-up and need your second dose. Ask whether they can give it if dose one was somewhere else, such as near your home.
6. **Bring proof.** Take photo ID showing your date of birth, details of dose one, and evidence you're a first-time student if they ask for it (for example, your university acceptance or enrolment email).
7. **Keep a record.** Screenshot or photograph any confirmation, so you can show you've completed the course.

### Moved away from home since dose one?

This is the most common situation: dose one near your family home in summer, and now you're living somewhere else. Whether you need to register with a GP near campus to get dose two is one of the details to confirm with the pharmacy or the official guidance. Even so, registering with a local GP is good practice for any student. It makes everything from prescriptions to urgent appointments easier.

### Studying in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland?

Most of the 2026 campaign information came from England-based sources (DfE, NHS England pharmacies). Health is devolved, so if you're at university in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland, check with your local health board, GP or university health service about what's available to you.

## MenB vs MenACWY: two different vaccines

People often mix these up. They protect against different strains, and having one doesn't cover you for the other. [Meningitis Now](https://www.meningitisnow.org/get-support/supporting-young-people/meningitis-in-students/) points out that MenACWY does not protect against MenB.

| | **MenB** | **MenACWY** |
|---|---|---|
| Protects against | Meningococcal group B | Meningococcal groups A, C, W and Y |
| Usually given | Babies (since 2015); 2026 catch-up for new students | At secondary school |
| Doses for students | Two doses in the 2026 catch-up | Usually a single jab |
| Free if you missed it? | Only if you're in the 2026 eligible groups | Yes, up to your 25th birthday ([UCAS](https://www.ucas.com/applying/after-you-apply/clearing-and-results-day/after-results-day/getting-ready-for-higher-education/vaccines-for-students)) |
| Where to ask | Pharmacy (free for eligible students); check GP or uni health centre | Your GP |

UCAS's "Starting uni or college in 2026?" checklist puts **two doses of MenB vaccine** first, with MenACWY also on the list. If you're unsure whether you had MenACWY at school, ask your GP. Not knowing is a perfectly normal reason to ask.

## For international students

- **Studying in the UK?** International students starting university for the first time are explicitly included in the DfE's eligibility wording. If you were born on or after 21 July 2001 and this is your first time at university, you're likely covered. Check the official page.
- **From Gibraltar?** Gibraltar runs its own [MenB catch-up programme](https://www.yourgibraltartv.com/daily/menb-vaccine-catch-up-programme-for-students) for 18–24-year-olds going abroad to study.
- **Heading to the US?** Many states require MenACWY for students living in campus housing. [Immunize.org keeps a state-by-state table](https://www.immunize.org/official-guidance/state-policies/vaccine-requirements/menacwy-college-2026/), current as of May 2026, so check it alongside your university's own requirements.

## Know the warning signs, whatever your vaccine status

Vaccines lower your risk, but they don't cover every type of meningitis. It can get serious quickly, and early symptoms can look like a hangover or flu. That overlap is exactly why it gets missed during Freshers.

- Read the symptom list on [Meningitis Now's student page](https://www.meningitisnow.org/get-support/supporting-young-people/meningitis-in-students/) now, before you need it.
- If you're worried about yourself or a flatmate, **don't wait to see if it passes**. In the UK, call 999 in an emergency, or NHS 111 for urgent advice.
- Check on friends who go quiet or stay in bed unusually long, especially after a night out.

## Quick FAQ

### Do I really need the second dose?

Yes. The catch-up is a two-dose course, and UCAS lists "two doses of MenB vaccine" for 2026 starters. One dose alone isn't the full course.

### Is the second dose free?

For eligible students on the 2026 catch-up, the jabs are free at pharmacies, according to The Conversation. Confirm with the pharmacy when you book, and check the official page that the programme is still running.

### Can I get dose two at a different pharmacy from dose one?

This may be possible, but confirm it. Call the pharmacy near your university, explain where and when you had dose one, and bring your record.

### I'm a postgrad. Can I get it?

Usually not. QMUL says the programme "typically does not include postgraduate students". If this is genuinely your first time at university, check the UKHSA eligibility wording.

### I had MenB as a baby. Do I need it again?

The Conversation says people vaccinated under the infant programme since 2015 should already be protected and aren't the main target. If you're unsure of your history, ask your GP.

## Next steps

Today, find your dose-one record and check the timing and programme details on the [UKHSA eligibility page](https://ukhsa.blog.gov.uk/2026/06/12/who-is-eligible-for-the-new-one-off-menb-vaccine-programme/). Then call a pharmacy near your university to book dose two. While you're at it, register with a local GP if you haven't yet, ask whether you've had MenACWY (free up to 25), and save the meningitis warning signs on your phone. It takes about 20 minutes and closes off one of the most avoidable risks of first year.
