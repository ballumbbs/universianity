If you're a first-year student in the UK and you want a career in banking, finance or law, the best opportunity open to you this autumn is probably a **spring week**. Most 2027 summer internships are already closing. Barclays' Banking Summer Internship 2027, for example, [closed on 23 October 2026](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/graduate-jobs/barclays/banking-summer-internship-programme-london-2027-alh7). Spring insight programmes for Easter 2027 are a different story. Many are open right now.

This guide covers what spring weeks are, who can apply, which programmes are confirmed open, and how to put together an application that stands out.

## What is a spring week?

A spring week, also called a spring insight programme, is a short programme run by a bank or financial firm. It usually lasts one week over the Easter break. It is designed for students early in their degree. You spend time inside the firm, learn how different divisions work, meet staff and build practical skills.

Trackers often describe spring weeks as the **earliest way into most banks**. They can also lead somewhere bigger. Standard Chartered's [US Spring Insight Programme listing](https://jobs.standardchartered.com/job/Spring-Insight-Programme-2027-US/63952-en_GB/) says strong performers will be "first in line" for its 2028 Summer Internship. Not every firm makes that promise, and the UK terms may differ, so check each programme's page. Still, it shows why so many students treat spring weeks as the first step towards an internship and then a graduate job.

## Who can apply?

Spring weeks are mainly for:

- **First-year undergraduates on a three-year degree.** For the 2027 round, that usually means you graduate in **2029**.
- **Second-year students on a four-year course.** This includes many Scottish degrees and courses with a placement or year abroad.

Some schemes go wider than this. One spring insight scheme [listed on TARGETjobs](https://targetjobs.co.uk/jobs/spring-insight-scheme-2027-252761) accepts first-year law and non-law students, plus penultimate-year non-law students. Eligibility differs from firm to firm, so read the criteria on each listing before you apply.

**International students:** you're studying at a UK university, but that doesn't mean every programme will be open to you. Right-to-work rules and whether a programme is paid vary between firms. Check the eligibility section of each official page, and contact the firm's graduate recruitment team if anything is unclear.

## Why applying early matters

Many UK spring weeks close between November and early January. The deadline on a listing isn't always the date that counts, though. Some firms review applications and make offers **as they come in**. That means places can fill up well before the official closing date.

In practice:

- If you apply in October or November, you're competing for the full set of places.
- If you apply on the last day, some of those places may already be gone.
- A strong application sent early beats a perfect one sent late.

Aim to have your first applications submitted within the next few weeks. Don't wait for the deadline.

## How to apply: a step-by-step plan

### 1. Build a shortlist (this week)

Choose about 8–15 programmes. Include a mix of large investment banks, boutique advisory firms like Rothschild & Co, and other financial firms like Macquarie or Standard Chartered. Put each one in a spreadsheet with these columns:

- Firm and programme name
- Link to the official listing
- Eligibility (year of study, graduation year, right to work)
- Deadline, or "rolling"
- Application stages
- Status (not started / drafted / submitted)

Bright Network's [financial services deadline list](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/application-deadlines/jobs/internships/financial-services/) can help you find more firms. Check each one at the source.

### 2. Get your CV ready

As a first-year, nobody expects you to have finance experience. Recruiters want to see that you're curious, reliable and able to take on responsibility. Keep your CV to one page and include:

- **Education:** your university, course, expected graduation year (2029 for most), and A-level or equivalent results.
- **Experience:** part-time jobs, volunteering, tutoring, society roles. Retail and hospitality jobs count. They show teamwork and dealing with customers under pressure.
- **Achievements:** competitions, investment or trading societies, projects, languages.

Use active verbs and concrete results wherever you can. "Trained three new staff members" is stronger than "helped with training."

### 3. Research each firm properly

Generic answers are easy to spot. For each firm, find out:

- What it actually does and which divisions the spring week covers
- Something specific that interests you, such as a recent deal, a market it's known for, or something distinctive about the programme itself (like Rothschild & Co's archive tours)
- How the programme fits what you want to learn

The firm's own careers page is the best place to start.

### 4. Write strong answers to the motivation questions

Most applications ask some version of "Why this firm?" and "Why this programme?" A simple structure works well:

1. **Hook:** what first got you interested in finance or this area. Make it a real, specific moment.
2. **Evidence:** something you've done that shows that interest, such as a society, reading, a project or a job.
3. **Firm fit:** two specific reasons why this firm, based on your research.
4. **What you'll gain:** what you hope to learn during the week.

Avoid repeating the firm's slogans back to it. Don't copy-paste answers between firms without tailoring them. Recruiters notice.

### 5. Prepare for the next stages

The stages differ by firm, and each listing should explain what to expect. After the written application, some firms use online assessments or a recorded video interview. Read the process on each official page. Practise answering out loud, and have a clear two-minute explanation of why you want to work in finance.

### 6. Track, follow up and keep going

Rejections are normal, even for strong candidates. Spring weeks are very competitive. Keep submitting applications. Each one gets faster and better.

## Don't forget the alternatives

If spring weeks don't work out, you still have options:

- **Summer internships open to first-years.** A few firms accept first-years for summer programmes. Trackr's timeline mentions examples such as First Wealth, which was expected to open in October 2026.
- **Diversity and insight days.** Some firms run shorter insight events for specific groups. These are worth checking on firms' student opportunity pages.
- **Law insight schemes.** If you're open to law, some spring schemes accept both law and non-law students.

## Quick FAQ

**Can I apply for a spring week if I'm in my second year?**
Usually only if you're on a four-year course. Standard Chartered's UK programme, for example, accepts second-years on four-year courses. Some law-related schemes accept penultimate-year non-law students. Check each listing.

**Do I need finance experience?**
No. Spring weeks are designed for students early in their degree. Show curiosity, motivation and transferable skills from jobs, societies or volunteering.

**Are spring weeks paid, and can international students apply?**
It depends on the firm. Pay and right-to-work requirements vary, so check the eligibility section of each official listing or ask the recruitment team.

**How many should I apply to?**
There's no fixed number. Around 8–15 well-tailored applications is a manageable target for most students. Quality matters more than volume.

**When is the Goldman Sachs spring week deadline?**
At the time of writing, trackers list it as "opening soon" with no confirmed date. Check goldmansachs.com/careers directly.
