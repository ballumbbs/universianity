If you applied to Oxford or Cambridge by the 15 October 2026 UCAS deadline for 2027 entry, the next big moment is the interview invitation. Invitations start arriving in November, and most interviews happen in the first three weeks of December.

This guide sets out what is officially confirmed, what has only been reported by tutoring sites (and needs checking), and what you can do now to be ready.

## The short version

- **Oxford:** shortlisted applicants will have **online interviews in December 2026**. Invitations usually arrive **between mid-November and early December**. Interviews **cannot be rearranged**.
- **Cambridge:** the main interview period is **7–18 December 2026**. Most invitations go out in **November**, some in early December. Depending on your college and course, your interview may be online or in person. If you miss it, you won't be considered.
- **Your job right now:** keep 7–18 December completely free, check your email (including spam) daily from mid-November, and start practising thinking out loud.

## Oxford: what's confirmed

According to [Oxford's official interviews page](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/applying/guide-for-applicants/interviews):

- All interviews for 2027 entry will be **online**, in **early to mid-December 2026**.
- Invitations come **by letter or email**, usually **between mid-November and early December**. Different courses send them on different days, depending on when their interviews are scheduled. If a friend on another course has heard and you haven't, that doesn't mean bad news.
- **Interviews cannot be rearranged.**
- Oxford publishes an **interview timetable for each subject**, linked from the official page. Check it for your course.

Oxford describes the interview as an academic conversation about your subject, a bit like a short tutorial. Tutors want to see how you think and how you respond to new ideas, and that often includes material beyond your school syllabus. It is not a test of how much you've memorised.

### Oxford: reported details (verify before relying on them)

Several tutoring sites have summarised Oxford's 2026 timetable and set-up. These points are useful for planning, but **check the official timetable and the [Oxford interview technology page](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/applying/guide-for-applicants/interviews/technology)** before you rely on them:

- Most interviews reportedly fall between **7 and 17 December 2026** ([The Profs' summary](https://www.theprofs.co.uk/student-resources/university-applications/when-do-oxford-interviews-take-place-2026-dates-and-timetable/)).
- Interviews reportedly run on **Microsoft Teams**, with a separate calendar invite for each interview. You reportedly don't need a Microsoft account.
- Courses are reportedly placed in technical **tiers**. Tier 1 is a video call only. Tiers 2 and 3 also use the **Miro** online whiteboard, for example for Maths and Physics problems.
- Applicants are reportedly advised to **switch off automatic transcription** in Teams.
- Course examples reported by third parties:
  - **Medicine:** 14 or 15 December
  - **History:** first-college interviews 7–11 December, with at least a week's notice. Second-college decisions by 11am on 14 December, for interviews 14–17 December
  - **Human Sciences:** 8–11 December
- Oxford reportedly gives about **20,000 interviews to about 10,000 shortlisted applicants**, so most people have more than one interview. They are often with two tutors and sometimes at a second college. For some courses the second-college interview is fixed in advance; for others it is decided during the week ([explainer on second-college interviews](https://www.leadingtuition.co.uk/oxbridge-interviews/second-college-interviews-oxford/)). Oxford reportedly aims to give **at least 24 hours' notice** of extra interviews.

## Cambridge: what's confirmed

From [Cambridge's official interview page](https://www.undergraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/apply/after/cambridge-interviews):

| Round | When |
|---|---|
| Main interview period | 7–18 December 2026 |
| Winter pool interviews | Around mid to late January 2027 |
| Mature applicants (January round) | Mid-March to early April |

- **Invitations:** most go out in November, some in early December. Cambridge says the timing of your interview **doesn't reflect how strong a candidate you are**.
- **Rescheduling:** only in exceptional circumstances, such as hospital appointments, illness, religious observance or exams. **If you miss your interview, you won't be considered.**
- **Number and length:** most applicants have **1 or 2 interviews totalling 35–60 minutes**. Some have 3 or 4, depending on subject and college. The number you get **says nothing about your chances**.
- **Format:** online or in person, depending on your college and course.

### Cambridge college examples

Cambridge colleges set their own arrangements, so **your college's applicant page matters most**. Two official examples:

**[Trinity Hall](https://www.trinhall.cam.ac.uk/study-with-us/undergraduate-study/applicants/)**
- Main interview period: **4–18 December**.
- UK-based applicants are interviewed **in person**. Applicants outside the UK are interviewed **online**.
- Invitations go out by email in **mid-to-late November**.
- Medicine interviews: **7, 9 and 10 December**.
- Some subjects have a **remote written assessment before interview**: Linguistics on 17 November, Architecture on 18 November, and MML, Music, AMES and History & Modern Languages on 19 November. For Classics, the assessment takes place at interview.

**[Jesus College](https://www.jesus.cam.ac.uk/college/undergraduate/how-apply/interviews-tests-and-written-work)**
- Interviews **7–18 December 2026** (dates provisional).
- Online or in person, depending on subject and whether you live in the UK.
- Jesus invites about **70% of its applicants** to interview.
- Written work was due in early November 2026.

Third-party sources report that Cambridge **Medicine and Music** interviews are all in person, that **Churchill** interviews everyone in person, and that **Peterhouse** and **Sidney Sussex** interview UK applicants in person and overseas applicants online ([college-by-college summary](https://www.leadingtuition.co.uk/blog/cambridge-interview-format-by-college-2026)). Confirm all of this on your own college's website.

## Oxford vs Cambridge at a glance

| | Oxford | Cambridge |
|---|---|---|
| Format | Online for all | Online or in person, by college/course |
| Main dates | Early to mid-December 2026 | 7–18 December 2026 |
| Invitations | Mid-November to early December | Mostly November, some early December |
| Rearranging | Not possible | Exceptional circumstances only |
| Typical load | Usually more than one interview, possibly at a second college | Usually 1–2 interviews, 35–60 minutes total |
| Second chance | Second-college interviews | Winter pool, mid to late January |

Neither format gives an advantage. Tutors are assessing the same thing either way: how you think.

- **UAT-UK test results:** reportedly **16 November 2026**.
- **Oxford decisions:** reportedly via UCAS on **12 January 2027**.

## How to prepare: a step-by-step plan

1. **Clear 7–18 December now.** Don't book trips, part-time shifts or anything else you can't drop. Oxford won't rearrange, and Cambridge only reschedules in exceptional cases. Tell your school if interviews might clash with mock exams.
2. **Check email and spam every day from mid-November.** You may get only about a week's notice. Add the university and college domains to your safe senders list.
3. **Set up and test your tech in the actual room.** For Oxford, follow the official technology guidance and practise with Teams and, if your course needs it, an online whiteboard. For Cambridge, use whatever platform your college specifies. Check your camera, microphone, lighting and internet connection, and keep a phone hotspot as a backup. If you need a quiet space, ask your school early.
4. **Re-read your personal statement and any written work.** Tutors may use them as a starting point. Be ready to go deeper on anything you mentioned. If you name a book, expect to discuss it.
5. **Practise thinking aloud.** Pick unfamiliar problems, passages, graphs or sources in your subject and talk through them out loud. Do it with a teacher, a friend, or just a voice recorder. Saying "I'm not sure, but my first thought is..." is far more useful to a tutor than silence.
6. **Get comfortable with not knowing.** Interviews are designed to push beyond your syllabus. Struggling is expected. What matters is how you use hints and change your mind when you get new information.
7. **Do a mock that matches your format.** If you're online, run it online. If you're in person, practise sitting across a table from someone. Ask for honest feedback on clarity, not on whether you got the "right answer".
8. **Plan the day itself.** Have water, paper and a pen ready, and know your timezone conversion if you're abroad. Log in early.

## If you're not invited

This is common, and it isn't a judgement on your worth. At Jesus, for example, about 30% of applicants aren't invited. At Cambridge, the **winter pool** gives some applicants another chance, with interviews in mid to late January. Whatever happens, check that your other UCAS choices are strong ones you'd be happy to accept.

## Quick FAQ

**Is an early or late invitation a good or bad sign?**
No. Oxford courses send invitations on different days, and Cambridge says interview timing doesn't reflect how strong a candidate you are.

**Do online interviewees do worse than in-person ones?**
There's no evidence for that. Tutors assess the same thinking skills in both formats.

**What if I'm ill on my interview day?**
Contact your college straight away. Oxford says interviews cannot be rearranged. Cambridge considers rescheduling only in exceptional circumstances, such as illness or hospital appointments.

**Does having more interviews mean I'm doing well?**
Not necessarily. Cambridge says the number of interviews says nothing about your chances. At Oxford, extra and second-college interviews are a normal part of the process.

**When will I hear the result?**
Third-party timelines give 12 January 2027 for Oxford and 27 January 2027 for Cambridge. Check the official pages to confirm.

By the time an invitation arrives, the format will feel familiar, and you can focus on the conversation itself.
