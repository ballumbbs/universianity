College Board calls the **5 December 2026 SAT** the "Last Test Before College Deadlines." If you're reading this on 24 October, you have about six weeks. That's enough time for a focused, structured push, as long as you start with a diagnostic test and register early.

This guide covers the dates, the costs and a week-by-week plan built on College Board's free official prep tools.

## Key dates at a glance

According to College Board, these dates and deadlines apply to **all students, in the US and internationally**, and every deadline expires at **11:59 p.m. ET**. Check the [official dates and deadlines page](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/dates-deadlines) before you act.

| What | Date (2026) |
|---|---|
| November SAT late registration deadline | Tuesday 27 October |
| November SAT test day | 7 November |
| November scores released | 20 November |
| **December SAT registration deadline** | **20 November** |
| December deadline for changes, regular cancellation and late registration | 24 November |
| **December SAT test day** | **5 December (7:45 a.m. local start, per the [College Board homepage](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/))** |
| December scores released | Friday 18 December |

After December, the remaining 2026–27 dates are **6 March, 1 May and 5 June 2027**.

## Is December the right sitting for you?

### Seniors applying Regular Decision

December scores come out on **18 December**. If any of your colleges have **1 January** deadlines, that is a tight window. Before you register, check each college's admissions website for how it treats December scores. If a policy is unclear, email the admissions office.

### Juniors taking a first sitting

December works well as a first attempt. You'll get a real score and still have March, May and June 2027 to improve.

### Still deciding about November?

The late registration deadline for the 7 November test is **27 October**, just three days away. Two weeks of prep is very little time, so for most people December is the more realistic target.

### If you need accommodations

The request and documentation deadline for the December test was **16 October 2026**, so it has already passed. College Board says approval can take **up to seven weeks**. If you need accommodations, aim for **March 2027** and submit your request well ahead of time. The [accommodations dates and deadlines page](https://accommodations.collegeboard.org/request-accommodations/dates-deadlines) lists the exact cut-off dates.

## What it costs

Fees can change, so confirm them on [How much is the SAT?](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/help-center/how-much-sat) and the [international fees table](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/registration/international-testing/fees).

| Fee | Amount |
|---|---|
| SAT registration | $68 |
| International registration fee (testing outside the US) | +$43, so **$111 total** |
| Late registration | $38 |
| Changing test centre | $34 |
| Cancelling | $34, or $44 after the change deadline |
| Test centre fee (some international locations only) | $24 |

If you're eligible for a **fee waiver**, you can take the SAT for free and receive additional benefits. Ask your school counsellor whether you qualify.

You can save $38 by registering before 20 November rather than in the late window. Do it in Week 1.

## What you're preparing for

The details below come from established prep guides such as [Applerouth](https://www.applerouth.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-the-digital-adaptive-sat) and [Scoir](https://www.scoir.com/blog/the-ins-and-outs-of-the-digital-sat). Confirm them on College Board's own page, [How the SAT Is Structured](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/structure).

- **Length:** about 2 hours 14 minutes, with 98 questions.
- **Two sections:** Reading and Writing, then Math. Each section has two modules.
- **Reading and Writing:** 27 questions per module, with 32 minutes per module. Two questions in each module are unscored experimental questions.
- **Math:** 22 questions per module, with 35 minutes per module. Two questions in each module are unscored.
- **Break:** one 10-minute break between the sections.
- **Adaptive design:** how you do on Module 1 determines the difficulty of Module 2.
- **Scoring:** each section is scored 200–800, for a total of 400–1600.

The adaptive design matters for strategy. A careful, accurate Module 1 sets you up for the harder second module, where higher scores are possible.

## Your free toolkit

You don't need to pay for prep. College Board's [practice hub](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice) brings together four tools:

1. **Bluebook™ full-length practice tests.** These are adaptive, timed and scored automatically, so you can see straight away what you missed.
2. **Paper practice tests.** These are downloadable and nonadaptive. They're useful for extra review but don't reproduce the adaptive experience.
3. **Student Question Bank.** It has thousands of official questions, and you can filter by skill.
4. **Official SAT Prep on Khan Academy.** Use it for skill-by-skill lessons and practice.

Khan Academy **no longer offers full-length practice tests** for the current SAT, as its [support forum](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/28733706410509-SAT-Practice-tests) confirms. Use Bluebook for full tests.

## The six-week plan

### Week 1 (24–30 October): Diagnose and register

1. **Register for 5 December now.** Pick your test centre and check the fee. International students should budget $111.
2. **Take one full Bluebook practice test** in a single sitting, timed, on the device you plan to use on test day.
3. **Start an error log.** This can be a spreadsheet or notebook. For every missed or guessed question, record:
   - the section and skill
   - why you got it wrong (content gap, misread, timing, careless slip)
   - the correct approach, in one sentence
4. **Rank your weak skills.** Pick the three to five that cost you the most points. These become your focus for Weeks 2–4.

### Weeks 2–4 (31 October–20 November): Targeted skill work

This is where most of your progress happens. Aim for short, regular sessions rather than occasional marathons.

- **Learn:** Use the Khan Academy lessons for your weakest skill.
- **Drill:** Do Question Bank sets filtered to that skill. Mix in easier and harder questions.
- **Log:** Add every miss to your error log.
- **Review with active recall:** Every few days, cover the answers in your error log and redo those questions from scratch. Spacing these reviews out works much better than rereading. Our active recall guide explains how to set this up.
- **Rotate:** Each week, add a new weak skill while keeping a little review going on earlier ones.

A simple weekly rhythm:

| Day type | Focus |
|---|---|
| 2 days | Math skills (Khan Academy and Question Bank) |
| 2 days | Reading and Writing skills |
| 1 day | Error-log review, redoing past misses |
| 1 day | Mixed timed set (one module's worth of questions) |
| 1 day | Rest |

**Deadline check:** The end of Week 4 is **20 November**, the regular registration deadline. If you haven't registered, do it before then to avoid the late fee.

### Week 5 (21–27 November): Second full test and pacing

1. **Take a second full Bluebook test** under the same conditions as your diagnostic. Compare the two section by section.
2. **Practise pacing for each module.** Based on the prep-guide timings, that works out to a little over a minute per Reading and Writing question and around a minute and a half per Math question. Notice where you rush and where you stall.
3. **Have a skip rule.** If a question is eating your time, make your best guess, flag it and move on. Come back if time allows.
4. **Update your error log** and spend the rest of the week on the patterns that showed up.

Any changes to your registration must be made by **24 November**.

### Week 6 (28 November–5 December): Light review and logistics

- **Days 1–4:** Short, light sessions. Redo error-log questions and do a few mixed sets. Don't start new topics.
- **Tech check:** Make sure Bluebook is installed and updated and your device meets the requirements. Complete any exam setup College Board asks for before test day.
- **Read the official rules:** Check [What to Bring on SAT Test Day](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/what-to-bring-do/what-to-bring) and [What to Expect on Test Day](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/what-to-bring-do/what-to-expect) for ID, admission ticket, device and calculator rules. Rely on these pages rather than second-hand checklists.
- **Day before:** No heavy studying. Pack your bag, charge your device, plan your route and sleep.
- **Test morning:** The start time is 7:45 a.m. local time, so arrive early.

## Quick FAQ

**Is six weeks enough to raise my score?**
It depends on your starting point and how consistently you study. Six weeks is enough to fix specific weak skills and improve pacing, which is exactly what this plan targets.

**Can international students take the 5 December SAT?**
Yes. College Board's dates and deadlines apply to US and international students alike. The total registration cost outside the US is $111 ($68 plus the $43 international fee), and some locations add a $24 test centre fee.

**When will I get my December scores?**
Friday 18 December 2026. If you have 1 January deadlines, check each college's policy on December scores.

**I need accommodations. Can I still test in December?**
The 16 October deadline has passed, and approval can take up to seven weeks. Aim for March 2027 and check the accommodations deadline page.

**Where do I find full-length practice tests?**
In Bluebook. Khan Academy offers skill practice but no full-length tests for the current SAT.
