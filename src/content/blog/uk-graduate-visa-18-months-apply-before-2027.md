If you're finishing a bachelor's or master's degree in the UK this year, there is one date you need to know: **31 December 2026**.

If you apply for the Graduate visa on or before that day, you get **two years** to live and work in the UK. If you apply on or after **1 January 2027**, you get **18 months**. That's six months of work experience, job hunting or time to switch to a sponsored role that you could lose because of when you click submit.

There are about nine weeks left. This guide covers what has changed, who is affected and how to give yourself the best chance of getting the full two years.

## What exactly is changing

The Graduate visa (often called the post-study work visa) lets international students stay in the UK after their course and work without an employer sponsoring them. From 2027 the length depends on your qualification and your **application date**:

| Your degree | Apply on or before 31 Dec 2026 | Apply on or after 1 Jan 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor's | 2 years | 18 months |
| Master's | 2 years | 18 months |
| PhD / other doctorate | 3 years | 3 years (unchanged) |

This isn't a rumour or a proposal any more. The cut was first announced in the Immigration White Paper on **12 May 2025**. It became law through a Statement of Changes to the Immigration Rules (**HC 1333**) on **14 October 2025**, as reported by [The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/shorter-graduate-route-from-january-2027-uk-govt-confirms/). A parliamentary written answer on **13 April 2026** repeated the 1 January 2027 start date, according to [Hextons Law](https://www.hextonslaw.com/post/graduate-visa-cut-to-18-months-from-january-2027-what-current-students-need-to-know).

[UKCISA](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/news/student-update-changes-to-the-student-and-graduate-rules/), the UK's advice body for international students, confirms that PhD graduates aren't affected and that applications made before 1 January 2027 aren't affected either.

## The mistake many students are making

A lot of students assume they're protected because they **started** their course before the change was announced, or because they **finish** in 2026. That isn't how the rule works.

These dates don't decide your visa length:

- When you enrolled
- When you finished your final exam or dissertation
- When you graduate or attend your ceremony

Only one date counts: **the day you submit your Graduate visa application.**

### The catch: you can't apply whenever you like

You can only apply **after your university has told the Home Office that you have successfully completed your course.** Until that report is made, an application will fail.

So if you submit your dissertation in September but your university doesn't report your completion until January 2027, you can't apply in 2026. You'll get 18 months.

This is why the next few weeks matter so much. Your deadline really depends on your university's reporting timeline, so you need to find out what it is.

## Who is most at risk

According to [UniPilot](https://unipilotai.com/news/uk-graduate-route-18-months-apply-before-2027) and university guidance, these groups need to pay the closest attention:

- **Master's students who started in September 2025.** Many finish dissertations in late summer or autumn 2026. Whether you get two years depends on whether your completion is reported before the end of December.
- **Students sitting autumn resits.** Results and completion reports may come late in the year.
- **One-year master's students who started in January or September 2026.** Most will finish in 2027, so they'll mostly fall under the 18-month rule.
- **Anyone starting a UK bachelor's or master's in 2026–27.** Plan your budget and career timeline around 18 months, not two years.

## Are you eligible? A quick checklist

Based on guidance from the [University of Leeds](https://students.leeds.ac.uk/visa-information-after-studies/doc/graduate-visas) and [DavidsonMorris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/graduate-visa-uk/), you need to meet all of these:

- You are **in the UK** and apply from inside the UK.
- You have, or last had, **Student (or Tier 4) permission**, and your current visa is **still valid** on the day you apply.
- You have **successfully completed** an eligible UK course at a higher education provider with a track record of compliance.
- You studied in the UK for **at least 12 months**, or for the whole course if it was shorter than 12 months.
- Your university has **reported your completion** to the Home Office.

You **don't** need to wait for your graduation ceremony or your degree certificate.

The Graduate visa **cannot be extended**. Whatever length you're granted is what you get, so the difference between two years and 18 months is permanent.

## How to apply in time: step by step

1. **Email your international student office this week.** Ask one question: *"When will you report my successful completion to the Home Office?"* Get a date if you can, and ask whether there's anything that might delay it, such as outstanding marks or exam boards.
2. **Check your Student visa expiry date.** If it ends before your university reports your completion, you won't be able to apply for the Graduate visa from inside the UK. Talk to your adviser straight away if the dates are close.
3. **Stay in the UK.** You must apply from inside the country. If you're planning a trip home over the holidays, make sure it doesn't clash with your application window. Ask your adviser before booking travel.
4. **Get your money ready.** You'll pay the application fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge upfront when you apply (see the costs below).
5. **Prepare your documents.** Have your passport and visa details ready, and your CAS number if your university advises you to include it. Your adviser can confirm what you need.
6. **Apply as soon as you become eligible.** Don't wait for the last days of December. Systems slow down, payments fail, and universities and advisers close over the holidays.
7. **Keep proof of your submission date.** Save your confirmation email and payment receipt.
8. **Wait for the decision in the UK.** You can stay in the UK while your application is being decided.

Check the official GOV.UK Graduate visa page for current processing times and for how your eVisa will work. One source, [Leap Scholar](https://leapscholar.com/questions/how-long-can-i-stay-and-work-in-the-uk-after-completing-my-masters-degree-if-i-apply-for-the-graduate-visa-on-or-before-31-december-2026), says the visa period runs from the date it's approved, but confirm this on GOV.UK rather than relying on it.

## How much it costs

The Graduate visa isn't cheap, and families or sponsors helping with costs should plan ahead. Figures from the [University of Strathclyde](https://www.strath.ac.uk/studywithus/internationalstudents/visasandimmigration/graduatevisa/) and the [University of Edinburgh](https://registryservices.ed.ac.uk/immigration/working-in-the-uk/after-studies/graduate-visa/costs-graduate-visa):

- **Application fee:** £937 for you, and £937 for each dependant.
- **Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS):** £1,035 per year, paid upfront in one payment.

| Visa length | IHS | Approx. total (main applicant) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 years | £2,070 | £3,007 |
| 3 years (PhD) | £3,105 | £4,042 |
| 18 months | Check GOV.UK | Check GOV.UK |

For the 18-month visa, working from £1,035 a year gives roughly **£1,552.50** in IHS. However, at least one university page shows a different figure that looks like a typo, so confirm the exact amount with the official GOV.UK IHS calculator before you budget.

One common confusion: the **£776 a year** IHS rate you may have paid before is the **student** rate for Student visa applicants. As [Strathclyde's IHS page](https://www.strath.ac.uk/studywithus/internationalstudents/visasandimmigration/immigrationhealthsurcharge/) explains, Graduate visa applicants pay the higher rate.

Fees change regularly, so check the official GOV.UK pages before you pay.

## Common reasons applications are refused

[Wesbridge Associates](https://www.wesbridgeassociates.co.uk/visas/work/graduate) and other advisers point to the same avoidable mistakes:

- **Applying from outside the UK.** You can't do it.
- **Applying before your university has reported your completion.** Wait for confirmation.
- **Letting your Student visa expire before you apply.** Once it's gone, so is your route.

With the deadline approaching, rushing can make these mistakes more likely. A refused application could push your next attempt into 2027, which means 18 months.

## Why the government made this change, and what it means for your job hunt

Sources describe the cut as a way to push graduates into **sponsored jobs** (the Skilled Worker visa) sooner. In practice, 18 months gives you less time to find a role, prove yourself and get an employer to sponsor you.

As [Jobbatical](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/uk-graduate-visa-18-months-2027-recruitment-impact-shortened) notes, employers that can't sponsor visas may lose graduates sooner. If you're on the 18-month visa, start targeting employers that can sponsor you from the beginning, and don't assume you'll have time to switch later.

## Quick FAQ

**I finished my course in 2026. Am I guaranteed two years?**
No. You need to **submit your application** on or before 31 December 2026, and you can only do that once your university has reported your completion.

**Do I need to graduate or get my certificate first?**
No. You don't need to wait for your ceremony or certificate. You only need your university to have reported that you successfully completed the course.

**I'm doing a PhD. Does this affect me?**
No. Doctoral graduates still get three years, whenever they apply.

**Can I extend an 18-month Graduate visa later?**
No. The Graduate visa cannot be extended. To stay longer, you'd usually need to switch to another route, such as a sponsored Skilled Worker job.

**Can I go home for Christmas and apply from there?**
No. You must apply from inside the UK while your Student visa is still valid. Plan any travel around your application.
