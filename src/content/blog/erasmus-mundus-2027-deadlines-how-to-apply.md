If you want a fully funded master's in Europe starting in September 2027, now is the time to choose your courses. Erasmus Mundus Joint Master (EMJM) applications are open, and many scholarship deadlines fall between **December 2026 and January 2027**. Some are earlier than that. For at least one well-known course, the scholarship deadline is **1 December 2026**.

This guide explains what the scholarship pays, how the deadlines work, which old rules you can ignore, and how to apply without missing a step.

## What Erasmus Mundus actually is

An Erasmus Mundus Joint Master is one master's programme run by a **consortium**, which is a group of universities, usually in several countries. You study at two or more of them, often in Europe and sometimes beyond. You graduate with a joint or multiple degree.

According to the [European Commission's official student page](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/opportunities/individuals/students/erasmus-mundus-joint-masters):

- **Students from anywhere in the world can apply.**
- Courses last **1–2 academic years** (60, 90 or 120 ECTS credits).
- You must have **completed your bachelor's degree before the course starts**. Final-year students can usually apply now.
- Full scholarships go to the **best-ranked applicants**. The scholarship covers your participation costs (tuition) and contributes to travel, visa and living costs.

There is no central application. You apply **directly to each course** you're interested in. Each consortium runs its own selection.

## How much the scholarship pays

Current guides that reflect the 2021–2027 programme agree on these figures:

- **€1,400 per month** for the full length of the course, including placements, thesis and defence periods.
- That works out at up to **€33,600** for a 24-month course. It's about €16,800 for 12 months and €25,200 for 18 months.
- **No tuition or other compulsory fees** for scholarship holders.
- **No application fee.** Erasmus Mundus courses are not allowed to charge one. If a site asks you to pay to apply, be careful.

**About travel money:** Several recent summaries, including [StudyU's overview of the 2026 rules](https://www.studyu.org/en/blog-erasmus-mundus-scholarships), say there is no longer a separate travel or installation allowance. Under this reading, travel, visa and setting-up costs come out of the €1,400 monthly amount. Check the scholarship section of your chosen course's website or the current Erasmus+ Programme Guide to confirm this before you plan your budget.

### Old figures you can ignore

You'll still find blogs quoting **"€1,000–€1,400 a month"** or **"up to €8,000 in travel grants"**. Those come from the 2014–2020 programme. Don't use them for budgeting.

## Old rules vs current picture

Many guides copy rules from before 2021. Here's what we can and can't confirm.

| Topic | What older guides say | Current picture |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly amount | €1,000 for some, €1,400 for others | €1,400 for all scholarship holders |
| Travel | Separate grant, up to several thousand euros | Reportedly included in the €1,400. Confirm with your course |
| Country categories | Different scholarships for "Partner" and "Programme" country students | One scholarship regardless of where you're from |
| Nationality limits | Not usually mentioned | Max **10%** of a course's scholarships to one nationality ([EACEA](https://www.eacea.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2022-12/EMJM%20-%20general%20presentation_Call%202023_final.pdf)) |
| Study in your own country | Often not funded | Scholarships now also paid for periods in your country of residence ([EACEA](https://www.eacea.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2022-12/EMJM%20-%20general%20presentation_Call%202023_final.pdf)) |
| "12-month rule" | Blocked if you lived in Europe 12+ months in the last 5 years | Not confirmed for 2027. See below |

### The "12-month rule": check before you rule yourself out

Many guides say you can't get a scholarship if you've lived or studied in Europe for more than 12 months in the past five years. That was part of the old Partner/Programme Country system. Some course pages still show it, such as [this international law master's scholarship page](https://master-international-law-erasmusmundus.eu/erasmus-mundus-scholarships/).

We couldn't confirm whether any residence rule like this applies for 2027 intakes. If you've spent time in Europe, **don't assume you're ineligible, and don't assume you're fine either.** Read the eligibility section of each course you're targeting. If it's unclear, email the course coordinator and ask directly.

### The "one scholarship only" rule

Several guides, including [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/erasmus-mundus-joint-masters-scholarship) and [OpportunityNest](https://www.opportunitynest.org/guides/erasmus-mundus-scholarship/#catalogue), say you can only hold an Erasmus Mundus Joint Master scholarship **once in your life**. If you've had one before, confirm the rule with the course before you apply.

### What each course decides

Each consortium sets its own:

- minimum grades and required subjects
- English (or other language) requirements and accepted tests
- documents, essays and number of references
- deadlines

This is why two Erasmus Mundus courses can have very different requirements.

## How the deadlines work

There is **no single Erasmus Mundus deadline**. Each consortium sets its own, and most for September 2027 entry fall between October 2026 and January 2027. Some are already gone. For example, MARIHE's first application round reportedly closed on 21 September ([TrueScho](https://truescho.com/en/blog/erasmus-mundus-2027-program-selection)).

The most common mistake is this: **a later self-funded deadline does not extend the scholarship deadline.** If you apply after the scholarship deadline, you'll only be considered as a fee-paying student.

### Example: Mundus MAPP (Public Policy), 2027–2029

These dates are reported by [Opportunities for Youth](https://opportunitiesforyouth.org/2026/08/27/erasmus-public-policy-2027/). Confirm them on the official course website.

| Round | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Scholarship applicants | **1 December 2026** |
| Non-EU/EEA self-funded | 15 April 2027 |
| Appeals | 20 April 2027 |
| EU/EEA self-funded | 1 July 2027 |

Mundus MAPP has **no separate scholarship form**. You're considered through the main application, as long as you submit it by the scholarship deadline. Other courses may ask you to tick a box or fill in an extra form, so read the instructions carefully.

### Typical timeline for 2027 entry

1. **Now – January 2027:** Scholarship deadlines
2. **February – May 2027:** Results, including reserve lists
3. **May – July 2027:** Accepting your place, visa applications
4. **September 2027:** Course starts

## How many courses are there?

Sources disagree. Recent guides give totals of **203, 218 or 219** courses in the catalogue. What's consistent is that **37 new courses** were selected on 16 July 2026 and will take their first students in September 2027. New courses are worth a look because fewer people know about them.

Use the live [EACEA Erasmus Mundus catalogue](https://www.eacea.ec.europa.eu/scholarships/erasmus-mundus-catalogue_et) for the current list. It's the official source.

## How competitive is it?

Very. Unofficial estimates put scholarship success rates at roughly **2–5%** ([Scholarship Union](https://guides.scholarshipunion.com/erasmus-mundus/)). Rates vary a lot between courses. Treat that number as a rough guide, not an official statistic. Because of the 10% nationality cap, applicants from countries that send many candidates are effectively competing with each other for a limited number of places on each course.

## How to apply: step by step

1. **Search the catalogue.** Filter the [EACEA catalogue](https://www.eacea.ec.europa.eu/scholarships/erasmus-mundus-catalogue_et) by subject and make a long list.
2. **Shortlist about three courses.** Pick courses that genuinely match your degree and goals. Each application takes real work, so a strong three usually beats a rushed ten. Check whether your courses limit how many Erasmus Mundus programmes you can apply to.
3. **Build a deadline sheet.** For each course, write down the scholarship deadline, the reference letter deadline (often different), required documents and the test-score rules.
4. **Check your English test date.** Many courses need scores taken within two years of the deadline. Mundus MAPP is one example. Book a test now if yours is old or missing.
5. **Contact referees early.** Give them at least three to four weeks, your CV, and a short note on why you're applying to each course.
6. **Gather documents.** Typical items include transcripts, your degree certificate (or proof you're in your final year), a passport, a CV and certified translations if needed.
7. **Write a course-specific motivation letter.** Explain why *this* consortium, *these* universities and *this* mobility path fit you. Generic letters are easy to spot.
8. **Submit early.** Portals slow down near deadlines. Aim to submit at least a few days before.
9. **Watch your email.** Some courses hold interviews. Reserve lists move, so a reserve-list place is not a rejection.

For a longer walkthrough, see [Scholarship Union's how-to guide](https://guides.scholarshipunion.com/erasmus-mundus/how-to-apply.html). If you're in Pakistan, the [Higher Education Commission's applicant guide](https://www.hec.gov.pk/english/HECAnnouncements/Documents/Guide-EMS.pdf) is also useful. Its date is unclear, so cross-check any figures in it.

## Quick FAQ

**Can I apply before I graduate?**
Yes, in most cases. You must have finished your bachelor's before the course starts, not before you apply. Check what proof each course wants.

**Does Erasmus Mundus cost anything to apply?**
No. Courses cannot charge application fees, and scholarship holders pay no tuition.

**Is there still a separate travel grant?**
Recent guides say no, and that travel and setting-up costs come out of the €1,400 monthly payment. Confirm with your course before you budget.

**I studied in Europe for a year. Am I blocked?**
Not necessarily. The old 12-month rule came from the pre-2021 system. Check the eligibility section of each course and ask the coordinator if it's unclear.

**Can I apply to more than one course?**
Generally yes, and shortlisting about three is common advice. Check whether any of your courses set their own limits.
