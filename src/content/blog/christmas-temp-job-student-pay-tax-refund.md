Shops, restaurants, warehouses and delivery firms are hiring Christmas temps now. The winter break is also when many students have the most free hours. That makes a seasonal job a good way to rebuild your bank balance before January.

It also makes it easy to get taxed too much. Short jobs often start on an emergency tax code, and lots of students lose money they never claim back. This guide covers what you should be paid, when tax and National Insurance actually apply, and how to get overpaid tax refunded.

You're not unusual for working. The HEPI/Advance HE Student Academic Experience Survey 2026 found that **65% of full-time undergraduates have a paid job**, working close to 14 hours a week on average ([MyStudentHalls](https://www.mystudenthalls.com/news/part-time-jobs-for-students/)).

## What you should be paid

Minimum wage rates change every April. These are the rates from **1 April 2026**:

| Your age | Legal minimum per hour | Previous rate (2025/26) |
|---|---|---|
| 21 and over (National Living Wage) | £12.71 | £12.21 |
| 18 to 20 | £10.85 | £10.00 |
| Under 18 and apprentices | £8.00 | — |

Sources: [Minimum Wage Rates](https://minimumwagerates.co.uk/) and [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/minimum-wage-uk/). Rates can change, so check the official GOV.UK page if you're reading this after March 2027.

A few things to watch:

- **Your birthday counts.** If you turn 21 during the job, your rate should rise to the higher band.
- **Unpaid trial shifts, unpaid training and "uniform deductions"** can drag your real hourly pay below the legal minimum.
- If you think you're being underpaid, the government's [Check Your Pay](https://checkyourpay.campaign.gov.uk/are-you-an-employer) campaign explains your rights.

## Do students pay tax? Yes, the same as anyone else

There's **no student exemption from tax** in the UK. HMRC taxes students like any other worker ([MyStudentHalls](https://www.mystudenthalls.com/news/part-time-jobs-for-students/)). What protects most students is the allowance everyone gets:

- **Income tax:** in the 2026/27 tax year (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027), the first **£12,570** you earn is covered by your Personal Allowance. If your total earnings for the year stay below that, you shouldn't end up paying any income tax.
- **National Insurance (NI):** this is calculated **each pay period**, not across the year. In 2026/27 it's due on earnings above **£242 a week**.

### Why the weekly NI threshold matters for Christmas jobs

Most students will earn well under £12,570 in a year. So for most of you, any income tax taken from a temp job is temporary and can be refunded.

NI is different. If you earn more than £242 in a single week, you may pay NI on the part above £242, **even if your yearly earnings stay under £12,570**. NI paid correctly doesn't come back as a refund.

Some quick maths at £12.71 an hour:

- **19 hours** = £241.49. That's just under the threshold, so no NI.
- **20 hours** = £254.20. NI is due on the £12.20 above £242.
- **30 hours** = £381.30. NI is due on £139.30.

This isn't a reason to avoid extra shifts. It just explains why your payslip may show NI deductions during busy Christmas weeks. NI contributions also count towards your National Insurance record. The [Low Incomes Tax Reform Group](https://www.litrg.org.uk/working/employment/national-insurance-employees/part-time-employment-national-insurance) has a clear guide to how NI works for part-time employees.

## How students end up overtaxed

The usual cause is the **tax code** your employer uses. Here's how it typically goes wrong. Check each step against GOV.UK, as these details come from general guidance rather than official pages.

### 1. The starter checklist

If you don't have a P45 from a previous job, your employer should ask you to fill in HMRC's **starter checklist**. You pick one statement:

- **A:** this is your first job since 6 April and you've had no taxable benefits or pensions. Most students with no other job this tax year choose this one.
- **B:** this is now your only job, but you've had another job since 6 April.
- **C:** you have another job or a pension as well as this one.

Picking the right box matters. If you pick the wrong one, or skip the form, you can end up on a code that takes too much tax.

### 2. Emergency tax codes

Common emergency codes include:

- **1257L W1 or M1:** you get your allowance, but each week or month is treated separately. A big Christmas week can be taxed even if your yearly total is low.
- **BR:** everything is taxed at the basic rate (20%), with no allowance at all.
- **0T:** no Personal Allowance, so everything you earn is taxed.

On a BR code, a £381.30 week could lose roughly £76 to income tax that you may not owe. [Xero's guide to emergency tax codes](https://www.xero.com/uk/guides/emergency-tax-code/) explains what each code means.

**Check your payslip on your first payday.** Your tax code should be printed on it. If it shows BR, 0T or W1/M1 and this is your only job, ask payroll whether your starter checklist was processed.

### 3. Two jobs at once

If you keep your term-time job and add a Christmas temp role, your allowance is usually applied to one job only. The second job is often taxed on BR. If your total earnings for the year stay under £12,570, that tax can usually be reclaimed.

## How to get overpaid tax back

There are three common routes. Confirm the current process on GOV.UK before you act.

| Situation | What usually happens | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| You're still working for the same employer | The overpayment is often refunded automatically through payroll later in the tax year | Check your payslips for a negative tax figure |
| You've stopped work and won't work again this tax year | You can claim mid-year | Use form **P50** online, through your personal tax account or the HMRC app |
| The tax year has ended (after 5 April) | HMRC may send a **P800** tax calculation | Follow the instructions to claim online or wait for a cheque |

### Step by step after your temp job ends

1. **Keep your P45.** Your employer should give it to you when you leave. It shows what you earned and the tax you paid.
2. **Add up your earnings** for the tax year so far, from all jobs.
3. **Compare them with £12,570.** If you're under it and you paid income tax, you're probably owed money.
4. **Sign in to your HMRC personal tax account** or the HMRC app to check your records and tax code.
5. **Claim** using the P50 route if you've stopped working, or wait for payroll or a P800 if you haven't.

[Save the Student's guide to student tax refunds](https://www.savethestudent.org/make-money/students-paying-too-much-tax.html) walks through these routes in more detail.

### Watch out for refund scams

Fake "you're owed a tax refund" texts and emails are common, and they often target people who've just changed jobs. Don't click links in unexpected messages about refunds. Go to GOV.UK yourself, or use the official HMRC app. You also don't need to pay a company to claim a simple refund, as the HMRC routes are free.

## International students: how much can you work?

If you're on a **Student visa**, your work conditions are part of your immigration permission. Breaking them can put your visa at risk. Check these rules on GOV.UK and with your university's international student team:

- Students on **degree-level courses** can usually work **up to 20 hours a week during term time**.
- You can usually work **full-time during your university's official vacations**. The Christmas break normally counts, but only the dates your university officially lists as vacation.
- Check the **exact conditions on your eVisa or BRP**, because limits depend on your course level.
- The Student visa **does not allow self-employment or gig-economy work**. That rules out being a freelance delivery rider, for example.

If your job runs into January, check when term restarts. Once term begins, the weekly limit applies again. [UKCISA's guide to working as an international student](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/student-advice/working/student-work/) is a reliable place to start. International students pay the same tax and NI as everyone else and can claim refunds the same way.

## Will a Christmas job affect your student finance?

For UK students, the Maintenance Loan is generally based on **household income**, not on what you earn from a part-time job. So your wages shouldn't reduce your loan for the current year. Confirm this with your student finance provider if you're unsure, especially if you're assessed as independent.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I pay tax if I only work over Christmas?**
Usually not overall, as long as your total earnings for the tax year stay under £12,570. You may still see tax deducted at first, but you can claim it back if you've overpaid.

**Why did I pay National Insurance when I earn so little?**
NI works week by week. Any week you earn over £242 in 2026/27, NI can apply to the amount above that, whatever your yearly total.

**I'm 19. Is £10.85 an hour legal?**
Yes. £10.85 is the legal minimum for 18 to 20 year olds from April 2026. Employers can pay more, and some big retailers do.

**Can I work 40 hours a week over the break on a Student visa?**
Often yes, if it falls within your university's official vacation and your visa allows full-time vacation work. Check your eVisa conditions and your university's vacation dates first.

**How long do I have to claim back overpaid tax?**
There are time limits, and they can change. Check GOV.UK and claim as soon as you know you've overpaid.

## Next steps

Before your first shift, make sure you know your correct hourly rate and fill in the starter checklist carefully. Most students with no other job this tax year will tick statement A. On your first payday, check your tax code, hours and NI on your payslip. When the job ends, keep your P45 and add up your earnings for the year. If you're under £12,570 and paid income tax, claim it back through your HMRC personal tax account or the HMRC app, and ignore any refund offers that arrive by text or email. If you're an international student, confirm your university's official vacation dates now, before you agree to extra shifts.
